**Do not describe this as a power ranking.** It measures play-by-play efficiency to date; it does not include opponent adjustment, injuries, betting markets, or preseason expectations.

## The lede in one paragraph

Kansas City is the Week 3 No. 1 at **86.7**, but Jacksonville made the biggest move among the contenders, gaining **18.7 index points** to rise from No. 7 to No. 2. Cincinnati, Chicago, and Atlanta also made major jumps. The counterpoint is just as useful: 3-0 San Francisco fell from No. 1 to No. 6 after a weak Week 3 efficiency performance, while Green Bay suffered the week’s largest drop. The index is still only three games old, so write the movement as a signal of early performance, not a settled statement about team quality.

## What the index measures

The Dual Eights Efficiency Index is the equal-weight average of four league percentiles:

1. Offensive EPA per play
2. Offensive success rate
3. Defensive EPA allowed per play
4. Opponent success rate

For the two defensive measures, lower is better before the percentile conversion. Each component is ranked across all 32 teams, converted to a percentile, then averaged into a 0–100 score. Ties share a rank.

Eligible plays are regular-season runs and passes with EPA. Kneels, spikes, two-point attempts, deleted plays, and aborted plays are excluded. The result is transparent and useful for comparing how well teams have played snap to snap, but it is **not opponent-adjusted**.

## Top 10 takeaways worth developing

- **Kansas City’s rise is the cleanest No. 1 case.** The Chiefs moved from fifth to first and gained 16.4 points. Their 3-0 record now aligns with the efficiency ranking.
- **Jacksonville is the headline mover.** The Jaguars added 18.7 points, the largest gain in the league, and moved into No. 2. The 35-6 Week 3 loss makes the placement surprising, which gives the article a built-in tension: this index rewards the complete three-week body of work rather than one result.
- **The 49ers are the key caveat.** They are 3-0 but dropped 14.9 points and five places after their Week 3 loss. That does not prove San Francisco is weak; it does show the early No. 1 position was fragile.
- **The Ravens are a useful fifth-place profile.** Their offense keeps them high, but the Week 3 win did not create a broad enough efficiency gain to hold No. 3. This can be a short sidebar or local angle rather than the centerpiece of a league article.
- **Records and efficiency have already separated.** The 3-0 Raiders are No. 11; the 3-0 Bills and Vikings are tied at No. 13; 0-3 Houston is No. 15. The article can make that contrast without claiming the standings are meaningless.
