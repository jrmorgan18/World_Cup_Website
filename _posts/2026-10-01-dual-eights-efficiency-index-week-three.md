After three weeks, a picture is beginning to emerge. It is still fuzzy, but the league has enough snaps on the books to identify teams that have consistently executed on one or both sides of the ball.

The Week 3 Dual Eights Efficiency Index has a new No. 1. Kansas City jumped four places to an 86.7 score. Jacksonville made the league's largest gain, climbing from seventh to second. Cincinnati and Chicago surged into the top seven, while the Ravens slipped from third to fifth despite winning in Brazil. San Francisco fell five spots despite remaining unbeaten.

Then there are the Bills.

Buffalo is 3-0, owns one of football's best offenses and sits at or near the top of most conventional power rankings. The Dual Eights Efficiency Index has the Bills tied for 13th.

That disagreement is useful. This is not a power ranking. It measures what teams have actually done on a play-by-play basis through three weeks.

## What the index measures

The score is the equal-weight average of league percentiles in offensive EPA per play, offensive success rate, defensive EPA allowed per play and opponent success rate. Defensive measures are reversed before the percentile conversion, so allowing less EPA and a lower opponent success rate help a team.

It does not adjust for opponent strength, injuries, preseason expectations or whether a team seems more dangerous than its performance so far. That makes it especially useful in September, when records, reputation and actual efficiency can tell very different stories.

### Kansas City has the cleanest No. 1 case

The Chiefs were 2-0 entering Week 3 but sat fifth at 70.3. Their 24-10 win over Miami added 16.4 index points and moved them into first. The index does not give Kansas City extra credit for its record or subtract anything for its opponent. Its first-place position comes entirely from its three-week efficiency profile.

At 3-0 and 86.7, Kansas City has the league's cleanest blend of results and underlying play.

### Jacksonville is the headline mover

Jacksonville produced the largest gain in the league, up 18.7 points from 64.1 to 82.8. The Jaguars' 35-6 win over New England was dominant by both the score and the underlying performance.

Three games are not enough to settle whether Jacksonville can sustain this, and the index does not adjust for schedule. It is enough to say that the Jaguars have earned the league's second-best three-week efficiency profile.

### The Ravens are fifth, and the questions are familiar

The Ravens won in Week 3 and still slipped two places. The 34-31 escape against Dallas was valuable in the standings and wildly entertaining: Lamar Jackson found Zay Flowers for 27 yards in the final seconds, then Tyler Loop made a 56-yard winner.

But the performance did not erase the concerns left by Week 2. The index fell from 73.4 to 71.1, a modest 2.3-point decline. The offense is doing enough to keep the Ravens among the league's leaders. The defense remains harder to trust.

Dallas repeatedly stressed a unit still looking for the consistency Jesse Minter expected. The Ravens survived because Jackson threw two touchdowns, added 50 rushing yards and created the final play that put Loop in range. Flowers' return restored a dimension that was missing against New Orleans, and the offense generated 34 points even as Derrick Henry averaged 3.4 yards per carry.

There is a clear early identity: when the offense is intact, the Ravens have enough firepower to remain one of football's more efficient teams even when other parts of the roster are uneven. The issue is that intact is becoming harder to assume after both Jovaughn Gwyn and Ethan Pocic left the Dallas game, leaving Vega Ioane to prepare for a start at center.

### The 49ers, Rams and Panthers show what the index can see

San Francisco is 3-0 but fell from first to sixth after its 36-30 win over Arizona pulled its cumulative efficiency closer to the rest of the league. That is not a verdict on the 49ers. It is a reminder that one game is still a third of the sample.

The Rams present the opposite record-versus-performance tension. They are 1-2 after losing 30-26 to Denver, yet their score improved slightly from 68.0 to 69.5. The losses count. So does the fact that their play-to-play profile looks much stronger than a typical 1-2 team's.

Carolina, at 1-2 and 10th, makes the same point. The index is not awarding playoff spots based on three weeks of success rate. It is simply saying the Panthers have played more efficiently than their record suggests.

## Why Buffalo is tied for 13th

Buffalo is 3-0 and tied for 13th at 57.0. That position does not mean the Bills are the 13th-most dangerous team in football. It means their defense is dragging down an otherwise elite offensive profile.

The Bills rank second in offensive EPA per play at +0.22. Josh Allen is fourth among quarterbacks in EPA per play, James Cook has been highly efficient and Buffalo has looked as dangerous with the ball as its reputation suggests.

On defense, though, Buffalo is 25th in EPA allowed per play at +0.075 and 27th in defensive success rate, with opponents succeeding on 47.2% of plays. The index gives each of its four components the same 25% weight. An elite offensive EPA percentile gets no extra influence over a poor defensive EPA percentile.

A human power ranking can reasonably incorporate Allen, the 3-0 record, preseason expectation and the likelihood that the defense improves. The index cannot. Its disagreement with the consensus is a limitation, but it is also what makes the measure useful.

## Biggest movement

Jacksonville owns the largest numerical improvement. Atlanta made the biggest jump in the standings, rising 14 places from 23rd to ninth after a 35-14 win at Green Bay. Chicago's 14.8-point gain is another movement worth tracking; three wildly different games have now been compressed into one early number.

Green Bay suffered the sharpest correction, down 19.5 points and 13 places after the Atlanta loss. The 49ers' 14.9-point decline is more revealing because it came in a win. The Jets, New England and Philadelphia all declined sharply, while Miami was already last and somehow created more distance at the bottom.

## Week 4 game of the week: Jaguars at Bengals

The index identifies a clear Week 4 matchup: Jacksonville at Cincinnati. The No. 2 Jaguars travel to face the No. 4 Bengals on Sunday.

Jacksonville carries an 82.8 index and Cincinnati sits at 72.7. Their combined 155.5 score is the highest on the Week 4 slate, while the 10.1-point gap is the fourth-smallest. It is an immediate test for two of the week's largest risers.

Kansas City at Las Vegas is another interesting contrast, with the No. 1 Chiefs facing a 3-0 Raiders team ranked 11th. Jets-Bears brings together one of the week's largest fallers and one of its largest risers. Locally, Tennessee enters Baltimore at No. 18 while the Ravens are fifth, though the offensive-line reshuffle is the major qualifier.

*Methodology: Regular-season runs and passes with EPA; kneels, spikes, two-point attempts, deleted plays and aborted plays are excluded. The data comes from nflverse schedules and play-by-play. The index is transparent and repeatable, but not opponent-adjusted.*
