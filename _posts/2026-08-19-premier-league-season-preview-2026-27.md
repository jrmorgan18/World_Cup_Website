The Premier League returns Friday with an unusual amount of uncertainty at the top.

Arsenal ended its 22-year title drought last season and enters as the deserved favorite to repeat. The champions return the manager, the core of the team and most of the structure that carried them to the trophy. They also benefit from the upheaval happening around them.

Pep Guardiola is gone from Manchester City, replaced by Enzo Maresca. Liverpool and Chelsea have new managers. Michael Carrick is now the permanent manager at Manchester United after rescuing last season. Nine clubs either have managers new to the league or managers who changed clubs, and 11 of the 20 teams will begin this season with a different manager than they had at the start of last season.

No contender can match Arsenal’s continuity.

Sunday’s 3-0 win over Manchester City in the Community Shield offered a glimpse of that advantage. Arsenal looked like a team that already understood exactly what it wanted to do. City looked like a talented group still learning a new version of itself.

That is only one of the stories heading into the season. Sunderland is trying to follow a promoted season that ended with a seventh-place finish and European qualification. Bournemouth and Brighton will also play in Europe. Newcastle has seen the core of its Champions League team torn apart. Coventry City is back in the Premier League for the first time in 25 years, with Haji Wright helping lead the way.

There is also a healthy group of USMNT players spread across the league, several of whom should have important club roles.

The season begins Friday, August 21, when Arsenal hosts Coventry City.

## How to Watch the Premier League in the United States

NBCUniversal owns the English-language Premier League rights in the United States. Matches will be shown across NBC, USA Network, NBC Sports Network and Peacock, with Spanish-language coverage available on Telemundo, Universo and Peacock.

Peacock will carry exclusive matches and simulcasts throughout the season, but it will not necessarily show every game live. Viewers should check the weekly schedule to see which matches are assigned to the television networks and which are exclusive to streaming.

The opening weekend begins with Arsenal–Coventry on Friday. Hull City hosts Manchester United on Saturday. Manchester City–Bournemouth and Newcastle–Liverpool headline Sunday, before Fulham hosts Chelsea on Monday.

## Americans to Watch

The current World Cup pool includes five Premier League players: Tyler Adams, Chris Richards, Antonee Robinson, Brenden Aaronson and Haji Wright.

There have also been persistent reports connecting Folarin Balogun with Tottenham, although no move had been completed at the time of writing.

### Tyler Adams — Bournemouth

Adams is healthy again and remains one of Bournemouth’s most important midfielders.

He had a strong World Cup for the United States, and the qualities that make him valuable internationally also matter for Bournemouth. His ball-winning, positioning and ability to cover ground allow more creative midfielders such as Alex Scott to take risks in possession.

Bournemouth is changing around him. Andoni Iraola left for Liverpool after guiding the club into Europe, and Marco Rose has taken over. European competition also adds a new layer of workload and rotation for a squad that has not had to manage it before.

For Adams, the challenge is less about proving he can play at this level than proving he can stay available through an entire season. A healthy year across the Premier League and Europe would do a great deal to erase the durability concerns that have followed him.

### Chris Richards — Crystal Palace

Richards probably has the most secure club role of any American in the league.

He established himself as a reliable center back and a major contributor to Palace’s recent trophies under Oliver Glasner. The situation is changing now. Glasner has left, Maxence Lacroix was sold to Chelsea and Pierre Sage inherits a team in transition.

Palace is still expected to retain a back three, a shape that suits Richards. His athletic and aerial ability has always translated. His continued improvement on the ball is the more important development, especially if the departures around him place more build-up responsibility at his feet.

### Antonee Robinson — Fulham

Robinson is already a proven Premier League player, but Fulham enters the season with considerable uncertainty.

Marco Silva has left and Álvaro Arbeloa has taken over. Fulham also lost Harry Wilson and Raúl Jiménez while trying to make the team younger.

Robinson’s width and progression from left back should remain central to the attack regardless of the system. The question is whether he can maintain his status as one of the league’s best left backs while the structure around him changes.

### Brenden Aaronson — Leeds United

Aaronson produced nine goal involvements in Leeds’ first season back in the Premier League and helped the club stay up.

Leeds improved dramatically after moving to a more direct 3-5-2 during the second half of the season. The club has since added goalkeeper James Trafford, defender Tarik Muharemovic and winger Harry Wilson while setting its sights on the top half.

That approach should suit Aaronson’s pressing, quick combinations and transition play. He is probably outside the first-choice USMNT lineup right now, but another productive Premier League season would keep him firmly in the pool.

### Haji Wright — Coventry City

Wright will get his first Premier League opportunity after helping Coventry earn promotion.

He scored 17 league goals and 18 in all competitions last season. Frank Lampard trusts his combination of size, speed and finishing, although a quad injury could keep him out of the opener against Arsenal. Coventry also added Taiwo Awoniyi to the forward group.

Once healthy, Wright should have a real opportunity. At 28, producing goals in the Premier League would be meaningful evidence that he can remain part of the USMNT picture through the 2030 cycle.

## Title Contenders

The preseason betting market begins with Arsenal at +120, Manchester City at +350, Liverpool at +550, Chelsea at +800 and Manchester United at +850. The prices vary by sportsbook and have already moved following the Community Shield, but the basic hierarchy is clear.

### Arsenal

**Preseason odds: +120**

Arsenal will spend this season defending a title after years of trying to prove it could win one.

The champions are the most stable team in the league and probably a stronger one than they were last season. Bruno Guimarães arrived from Newcastle for $101.5 million and should pair with Declan Rice in a midfield that can win the ball, carry it through pressure and give Martin Ødegaard more freedom. Christos Tzolis adds another attacking option after Leandro Trossard’s departure.

There are some defensive injury questions, but Arsenal still has the league’s best combination of defensive talent, depth and continuity.

The Community Shield was only one game, but the 3-0 result was encouraging. Arsenal knew its plan. City was still learning one.

Arsenal is the deserved favorite.

### Manchester City

**Preseason odds: +350**

The biggest story in Manchester is the manager.

Guardiola is gone after a decade, and Maresca offers some philosophical continuity without being a direct replacement for the person who defined the club.

The turnover goes beyond the sideline. Bernardo Silva, John Stones, Manuel Akanji and Nathan Aké have all left. Rodri joined Barcelona after leading Spain at the World Cup. That is an enormous amount of institutional knowledge removed from one team.

City responded by paying a British-record $157 million for Elliot Anderson from Nottingham Forest. Anderson is an excellent midfielder, but replacing Rodri is not simply a matter of buying another excellent midfielder. Rodri controlled games, protected counters and allowed City to play almost every match on its preferred terms.

Maresca is replacing both the on-field controller and the sideline controller at the same time.

Erling Haaland remains, and the talent around him is still overwhelming. Jérémy Doku and Rayan Cherki should continue improving. City belongs in the title race, but it is rebuilding the spine of the team while also replacing the most influential manager in the league.

### Liverpool

**Preseason odds: +550**

Liverpool’s post-Jürgen Klopp transition has been less smooth than it initially appeared.

Arne Slot won the title in his first season and finished fifth in his second. Liverpool then hired Iraola from Bournemouth, a move that makes stylistic sense. His teams are aggressive without the ball, attack quickly after turnovers and are comfortable creating disorder.

He also inherits a roster that has changed dramatically.

Mohamed Salah, Andy Robertson and Ibrahima Konaté are gone. Liverpool added Jérémy Jacquet, Victor Muñoz and Ronald Araújo on loan, but there is no straightforward replacement for Salah’s production.

The attack should be better positioned to integrate Alexander Isak, while Florian Wirtz can become the creative centerpiece. The question is whether Iraola can organize all of the new relationships quickly enough.

Liverpool has enough talent to win the league. It also has considerably more to discover about itself than Arsenal does.

### Chelsea

**Preseason odds: +800**

Chelsea remains the hardest team in the league to predict.

The club finished tenth and changed managers again, hiring Xabi Alonso after his brief Real Madrid tenure. Alonso’s best Bayer Leverkusen teams used a positional attack and fluid shapes to create overloads. Chelsea has given him a huge collection of talented players. The question is whether he can organize all of it.

Morgan Rogers arrived for a British-record $158 million. Chelsea also added Marco Palestra, Lacroix, Geovany Quenda and several others, while Danny Welbeck and Jordan Henderson bring some badly needed veteran experience.

The most important structural advantage may be the absence of European soccer. Alonso will have full training weeks while Arsenal, City and Liverpool manage continental schedules.

A title may still be too much to expect, but the talent and schedule make Chelsea at +800 interesting.

### Manchester United

**Preseason odds: +850**

Manchester United is the most plausible outside contender.

Carrick went 12-3-2 in 17 matches after taking over last season, the best record in the league during that span, and lifted United to third. He has now been given the permanent job.

There is danger in simply extrapolating that short run over a full season. Carrick did, however, make the team easier to understand. He moved away from the confusion of the Rúben Amorim era, returned to a back four and gave players more conventional roles.

United added Andrey Santos and Youri Tielemans in midfield while Casemiro and Rasmus Højlund departed. An attack built around Bruno Fernandes, Matheus Cunha, Bryan Mbeumo and Benjamin Šeško should create chances.

The defense and the sustainability of Carrick’s early success are the bigger questions. United starts below Arsenal, City and Liverpool, but the gap is smaller than it has been in recent years.

## Who Could Be This Year’s Sunderland?

Sunderland shocked the league’s hierarchy last season, turning promotion into a seventh-place finish and a Europa League place. Several clubs outside the traditional contenders could make a similar push toward Europe.

### Brighton

Brighton is the best team outside the obvious group to challenge for a Champions League place.

Its chance creation already approaches elite territory. Only the two Manchester clubs scored more goals from open play last season, and Brighton’s open-play expected goals matched Arsenal. Consistency was the problem.

Jan Paul van Hecke left for Tottenham and Welbeck is gone, but Luka Vušković and Pascal Struijk should help the defense, while Charalampos Kostoulas and Stefanos Tzimas were added to replace goals.

American-born manager Fabian Hürzeler is entering his third season and has more continuity than most of the coaches around him. The Conference League workload is the concern. Brighton’s previous European season contributed to an 11th-place league finish.

A top-six finish is realistic. A Champions League place is possible.

### Brentford

Brentford nearly produced a Sunderland-type season last year. The club entered the final day with a chance to reach the Europa League, drew Liverpool and finished ninth on goal difference.

Unlike several direct rivals, Brentford does not have European matches to manage.

Igor Thiago scored 22 Premier League goals, trailing only Haaland, and Callum Wilson has been added as another forward option. Mamadou Sangaré arrives from Lens after making the Ligue 1 team of the season and looks like a natural fit for Brentford’s press.

The club’s replacement model continues to work. A Europa League place would not be surprising. The Champions League would require a much larger leap.

### Leeds United

Leeds is the closest comparison to Sunderland.

The final 14th-place finish was misleading. After switching to a 3-5-2 in December, Leeds produced the league’s seventh-best record over the rest of the season.

The club has reinforced that structure. Trafford arrived for a club-record $54 million, Muharemovic cost $46 million and Wilson adds proven Premier League creativity. Leeds also scored 16 set-piece goals last season.

The concern is an attack that produced only 26 open-play goals. The second-half evidence is still meaningful: Leeds played at a European pace for half a season.

A Champions League place would be a shock. Finishing seventh or eighth would not.

## Other Storylines

### What Happens After Pep?

Guardiola’s City set the tactical standard for the league for most of the last decade. The attempts to copy and counter City helped drive the broader evolution of English soccer: building from the back, coordinated pressing, counterpressing and increasingly sophisticated positional attacks.

Now the league’s central tactical foil is gone.

Will Arsenal’s model set the next direction, or will the new managers — Iraola, Alonso, Rose and others — pull the league somewhere different?

### Newcastle Tears It Down

Eddie Howe resigned. Guimarães joined Arsenal. Sandro Tonali moved to Tottenham. Anthony Gordon left for Barcelona.

That is the core of a Champions League contender disappearing in one summer.

Matthias Jaissle inherits a group of expensive young replacements and the beginning of a new cycle. Newcastle may still be good, but this is no longer the same team that climbed into the league’s top four.

### Sunderland Tries Again, This Time in Europe

Sunderland was the best story in the league last season, going from promotion to seventh place and the Europa League.

The difficult part begins now. The summer has been quieter than the previous one, and the Thursday-Sunday schedule has damaged deeper squads than Sunderland’s.

Bournemouth faces a similar question. Both clubs now have to show that a magical season can become something sustainable.

### Can the Promoted Clubs Survive?

Coventry, Ipswich Town and Hull City replace West Ham, Wolves and Burnley.

Before Leeds and Sunderland survived last season, six consecutive promoted clubs had gone straight back down across the 2023-24 and 2024-25 seasons. Over the last decade, more than half of the promoted teams were relegated in their first season.

All three newcomers have spent money.

Coventry won the Championship under Lampard and has Wright leading the attack. Ipswich spent more than $135 million and hired Gary O’Neil. Hull faces the hardest task of the three.

## Prediction

It is difficult to pick against Arsenal after a season in which it came close to a Premier League and Champions League double, then added Guimarães to an already excellent midfield.

The race should be more open than the final margin last season suggests. Liverpool, Manchester United and Chelsea all have plausible paths back into contention, and the turnover among the traditional powers could allow Bournemouth, Brighton, Brentford or Palace to steal a top-five place.

But Arsenal won the league comfortably, got stronger and enters with fewer structural questions than every major rival.

**Premier League champion: Arsenal**
