*Updated after games completed August 26. Baltimore is 65-68 with 29 games remaining.*

Somehow, the Orioles are still hanging around the Wild Card race.

They spent July selling off major-league players, traded Adley Rutschman to a division rival and entered the final five weeks below .500. Yet the American League has left a door open. The question is simple: how many games do the Orioles actually need to win to make the playoffs?

The answer is probably around 83. A custom, schedule-aware simulation makes 83 the single most likely final total for the third American League Wild Card. But that does not mean an 83-win Orioles team is safe. Baltimore has already lost season-series tiebreakers to Texas and Seattle. At 84, the odds begin to shift meaningfully in its favor.

That makes **84 wins the practical target.**

## The Wild Card race is bad, but the Orioles’ task is not easy

Through August 26, Baltimore was 65-68 with 29 games remaining. Normally, a team three games below .500 at this point would be evaluating prospects and figuring out tee times. Not this year.

Cleveland is 68-66, Texas 66-68, Toronto 65-69, Minnesota and Seattle are both 64-70, and Baltimore is 65-68. The division races add another complication: Houston leads the AL West at 66-67, while the White Sox lead the Central at 70-63. The eventual runners-up in those races will join the Wild Card pool.

The good news is that no obvious 90-win juggernaut is looming. The bad news is that there are a lot of teams close enough to matter.

## The projection systems point toward the low 80s

FanGraphs’ updated schedule-aware model projects Chicago to finish at 83.6 wins and Cleveland at 82.9. Houston, Seattle and Texas cluster around 80 to 81, while Toronto and Minnesota project to 79.1 and 78.3. The Orioles project to 79.4.

The exact Baltimore number is not the point. Several teams are expected to land between 79 and 82 wins, and over roughly 30 games, one or two can easily finish two or three victories above their projection. Different FanGraphs models tell the same basic story: the O’s are roughly a 79-win team if everyone performs as expected. They need to beat that expectation by about four wins to create a legitimate playoff position.

## 83 is the most likely cutoff

To get beyond projected standings, I ran a one-million-season Monte Carlo simulation of the remaining American League schedule. Each team’s actual record through August 26 served as the starting point; FanGraphs Depth Charts rest-of-season winning percentages supplied the talent baseline. The model simulated the actual remaining games, including direct contender matchups and a modest home-field advantage, then assigned division titles and the three Wild Cards.

The mean Wild Card cutoff was 82.92 wins. The median was 83. The mode was 83.

That is considerably lower than the mid-to-high 80s total normally associated with playoff contention. The American League deserves the credit for that, or the blame. Take your pick.

## Why 83 is not safe enough for the Orioles

An 83-win cutoff does not necessarily mean an 83-win Orioles team gets in. Rather than randomly award tied Wild Card spots, the simulation separates outcomes where Baltimore is outright above the line, tied at the line and below it.

Texas owns the season-series tiebreaker, four games to two, and Seattle owns it as well, four games to three. There is no Game 163. If the Orioles and Rangers finish tied for the final spot, Texas advances; the same is true in a two-team tie with Seattle.

Cleveland leads the season series 3-1 with three games left, so Baltimore would need a sweep to win that tiebreaker. Toronto leads 4-3 with six games remaining, meaning the Orioles need at least four of those six to take the head-to-head edge. Eighty-two wins keeps the club alive. Eighty-three makes it outright clear of the line more often than not. Eighty-four provides real breathing room.

## Cleveland and Seattle are the biggest threats

If there is a reason this race climbs toward 83 or 84 rather than collapsing to 80 or 81, schedule strength is probably it.

Cleveland is particularly dangerous. The Guardians are 68-66, project as a .507 team the rest of the way and have several direct games against competitors. They still play Kansas City, the Athletics and Angels, as well as Toronto, Detroit, Baltimore and Minnesota. Every Cleveland win in one of those games is automatically a loss for another contender.

Seattle may be even more interesting. The Mariners are only 64-70, but the projections put them at a .549 team the rest of the way, the strongest rate among the bubble clubs. Their closing slate also includes the Athletics, Angels and Rockies. Seattle has played badly enough that Baltimore cannot simply assume that projection comes true, but it is exactly the kind of schedule that can turn an apparently dead team into an 82- or 83-win club. The Mariners also own the tiebreaker.

## Toronto might be the sleeper

FanGraphs is not especially bullish on the Blue Jays, projecting them around 79 wins, but their remaining six games against Baltimore are enormous. Toronto currently leads the season series 4-3. Every Orioles win in those games improves Baltimore’s record, damages Toronto’s and could flip the first postseason tiebreaker.

The O’s may get the chance to eliminate one of their competitors themselves. They just have to remain alive when those games arrive.

## Which brings us to the Orioles’ schedule

The remaining schedule was almost perfectly divided in two when this analysis began. First came nine games against St. Louis, Oakland and Colorado. Then the final 22 came against Boston, Cleveland, Milwaukee, Toronto, the Mets and Yankees.

Before the St. Louis series, Baltimore needed to finish 21-10 to reach 84. A 7-2 record against St. Louis, Oakland and Colorado would reduce the final demand to 14-8 against the much tougher group. Go only 5-4 against those three weaker opponents and the Orioles would need to play .727 baseball over the final 22 just to reach 84.

The first two wins in St. Louis were therefore more than another pair of victories. Baltimore is now 65-68 with 29 games remaining: 18-11 gets to 83, and 19-10 gets to 84. They have started the soft portion 2-0. Now they need to keep banking wins.

## So what do the Orioles actually need?

- **81 wins:** Needs substantial help.
- **82 wins:** Live, but likely needs a favorable standings or tiebreaker outcome.
- **83 wins:** The most likely WC3 threshold and a legitimate playoff position.
- **84 wins:** Probably enough and the preferred target.
- **85 wins:** A very strong position.

For the Orioles, 84 is the number to target. That still means a .655 winning percentage over the final 29 games for a team that has spent almost the entire season below .500. Nobody should mistake this for an argument that Baltimore is suddenly likely to make the playoffs; betting markets still price the club around +500 to make the field.

But those are sunk costs now. This bizarrely weak American League has left one final opening. The Orioles do not need to become a 100-win-caliber team, and they probably do not need the 87 or 88 wins normally associated with a Wild Card. After everything that has happened this season, 84 is still within reach.

Now they need to stay hot and win the games.
