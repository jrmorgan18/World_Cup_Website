<p data-source-paragraph="2"><strong>November 18, 2026 | Through Week 10</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="3">The Jacksonville Jaguars are still the most efficient team in football. But the competition is getting more interesting.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="4">Jacksonville maintained its position atop the Dual Eights Efficiency Index following a comfortable 40-23 victory over Tennessee. The Jaguars now hold a relatively narrow 1.15-point advantage over San Francisco, with the Los Angeles Rams climbing into third after their fourth consecutive victory.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="5">Our forward-looking Dual Eights Power Index (DEPI) tells a slightly different story.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="6">San Francisco remains the team to beat, but the Rams have closed to within just 0.21 projected points of the 49ers. Cincinnati and Detroit are also making strong cases that they belong among the league&#x27;s legitimate contenders after impressive victories over Pittsburgh and New England.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="7">The Rams, Bengals and Lions produced the three largest improvements in Efficiency this week, and those gains were supported by meaningful progress on both sides of the football.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="8">Meanwhile, the Ravens lost their fifth consecutive game, this time to a Chargers team that hadn&#x27;t won all season. Yet they actually climbed four places in DEPI, even as its Efficiency rating declined.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="12"><strong>1. Jacksonville Jaguars — 67.61</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="13"><strong>7-2 | Previous rank: 1 | DEPI: 6</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="14">Jacksonville continues to demonstrate the kind of balance that makes a team difficult to dislodge from the top of an efficiency ranking.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="15">The Jaguars defeated Tennessee 40-23 behind another outstanding performance from Trevor Lawrence, who threw for 352 yards and three touchdowns. The offense generated +0.206 EPA per play while the defense allowed -0.020.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="16">That is an excellent overall performance, although the opponent-adjusted picture was slightly less impressive. Jacksonville&#x27;s adjusted offensive gains were offset by a modest defensive decline, leaving its Efficiency rating essentially unchanged.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="17">The bigger concern comes from DEPI, where the Jaguars have slipped from fourth to sixth.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="18">Jacksonville still projects as a strong team at +4.77, but its lead in actual Efficiency is no longer translating into a similarly dominant forward-looking projection.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="19"><strong>2. San Francisco 49ers — 66.46</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="20"><strong>8-1 | Previous rank: 3 | DEPI: 1</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="21">The 49ers scored 46 points against Dallas and needed almost every one of them.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="22">San Francisco&#x27;s 46-40 victory was an extraordinary offensive performance, producing 584 total yards and +0.439 EPA per play. Christian McCaffrey scored three touchdowns, Deebo Samuel returned with a 70-yard rushing touchdown, and Brock Purdy threw for 389 yards.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="23">The offense is simply creating too many problems for opponents. However, allowing 40 points and +0.196 EPA per play defensively is hardly reassuring.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="24">That imbalance is visible in DEPI. San Francisco&#x27;s projected offense contributes an outstanding +5.91 points, while its defense contributes just +0.42.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="25">The 49ers remain the model&#x27;s strongest team at +6.76, but they are increasingly dependent upon their offense being exceptional rather than merely good.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="26">That&#x27;s a reasonable formula with this group. It also creates opportunities for opponents who can keep pace.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="27"><strong>3. Los Angeles Rams — 63.24</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="28"><strong>6-4 | Previous rank: 5 | DEPI: 2</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="29">The Rams may be the most interesting team in the NFL right now.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="30">Four consecutive victories have completely changed their statistical profile, culminating in a 34-6 demolition of Arizona..</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="31">Los Angeles generated +0.255 offensive EPA per play while holding the Cardinals to an astonishing -0.521. The defense supplied the largest contribution to a 3.55-point improvement in the Efficiency Index, the biggest gain of any team this week.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="32">The offensive performance was particularly encouraging considering the absences of Puka Nacua and Davante Adams. Matthew Stafford still threw three touchdown passes to three different receivers.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="33">Earlier in the season, DEPI was considerably more optimistic about the Rams than their actual results justified.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="34">Now the performance is catching up with the projection.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="35">Los Angeles ranks third in Efficiency and second in DEPI at +6.56, with strong contributions from both offense (+4.49) and defense (+2.56).</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="36">The Rams enter their bye playing some of the best football in the league. Their 6-4 record doesn&#x27;t completely capture how dangerous they&#x27;ve become.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="37"><strong>4. Chicago Bears — 63.18</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="38"><strong>7-2 | Previous rank: 2 | DEPI: 3</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="39">Chicago slipped two positions during its bye week, as the Rams&#x27; improvement pushed Chicago down the rankings, with just 0.06 points separating the two teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="40">DEPI remains extremely optimistic about the Bears, placing them third at +5.39. Their projected offense contributes +3.70 points, providing a strong foundation as Chicago prepares to face New Orleans.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="41"><strong>5. Cincinnati Bengals — 61.42</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="42"><strong>7-2 | Previous rank: 8 | DEPI: 4</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="43">Joe Burrow and Ja&#x27;Marr Chase dismantled the Steelers on Sunday night.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="44">Burrow threw for 409 yards and four touchdowns. Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards. Cincinnati won 38-18 and generated an outstanding +0.368 offensive EPA per play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="45">The Bengals have now won five consecutive games, and the statistical improvements extend beyond their passing offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="46">Cincinnati&#x27;s adjusted defense improved as well, helping produce a 2.92-point Efficiency gain. DEPI increased by 1.04 points, the largest improvement of any team this week.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="47">The model now places Cincinnati fourth at +5.27.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="48">The offense accounts for +4.72 of that rating, while the defense remains slightly negative at -0.69. That is still an imbalance, but a substantially more manageable one than earlier in the season.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="49">The Bengals have become one of the AFC&#x27;s most dangerous teams. Their defensive improvement will determine whether they&#x27;re merely capable of winning a shootout against anybody or can consistently control games against the league&#x27;s best opponents.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="50"><strong>6. Detroit Lions — 59.78</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="51"><strong>7-2 | Previous rank: 9 | DEPI: 5</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="52">Detroit&#x27;s 35-10 victory over New England in Munich was one of the most complete performances of Week 10.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="53">The Lions generated +0.236 offensive EPA per play while allowing -0.264 defensively.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="54">Jahmyr Gibbs produced 252 yards from scrimmage, Amon-Ra St. Brown caught two touchdowns, and Detroit finished with 494 total yards.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="55">The defense was just as impressive statistically, limiting New England to 151 yards.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="56">There is an important qualification. Patriots quarterback Drake Maye left the game with an ankle injury, forcing New England to play much of the afternoon with Tommy DeVito. That makes the defensive performance somewhat less persuasive than the raw numbers alone suggest.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="57">Still, Detroit deserves credit for taking advantage and dominating an opponent that entered the week with a winning record.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="58">The Lions improved 3.46 Efficiency points on consistent data, second only to the Rams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="59">More importantly, their projected defense has finally moved into positive territory in DEPI, complementing an offense worth +3.80 points. Though they will now be without Aidan Hutchinson for the rest of the season after he injured himself celebrating a sack.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="60">Detroit now ranks fifth in DEPI at +4.81. The offense has been strong for some time. If the defensive improvement continues against healthier opponents, the Lions have a legitimate path toward joining the top tier.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="61"><strong>7. Kansas City Chiefs — 59.24</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="62"><strong>7-2 | Previous rank: 7 | DEPI: 8</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="63">Kansas City handled Atlanta 35-13 behind a vintage Patrick Mahomes performance.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="64">Mahomes threw for 348 yards and five touchdowns, while rookie Emmett Johnson contributed 171 yards from scrimmage in place of the injured Kenneth Walker.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="65">The defense was particularly impressive, allowing -0.268 EPA per play. That helped the Chiefs improve their Efficiency rating by 0.59 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="66">DEPI remains somewhat skeptical, placing Kansas City eighth at +2.20. The model gives the Chiefs credit for a solid defense (+0.82), but special teams remain a meaningful drag at -0.92.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="67">A 7-2 team quarterbacked by Mahomes is obviously dangerous. The question is whether Kansas City is playing at the level we&#x27;ve come to expect from a serious championship contender.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="68">Our forward-looking numbers aren&#x27;t entirely convinced.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="69"><strong>8. Seattle Seahawks — 58.23</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="70"><strong>6-4 | Previous rank: 10 | DEPI: 9</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="71">Seattle returned to the top eight after defeating Las Vegas 21-13.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="72">Drew Lock threw for 240 yards and two touchdowns, and the Seahawks defense completely shut down the Raiders after halftime, allowing just 27 second-half yards.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="73">Both offensive and defensive efficiency improved, producing a 1.68-point overall gain.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="74">Seattle also returns to the DEPI top 10 at +1.91, although its projection assumes Sam Darnold returns as the starting quarterback.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="75">That remains a relevant qualification. The Seahawks are optimistic about his return following their bye, but the expected lineup needs to become reality before the projection can be treated as settled.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="76"><strong>9. Buffalo Bills — 58.12</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="77"><strong>8-1 | Previous rank: 6 | DEPI: 7</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="78">Buffalo fell three places despite beating the Jets 31-12. James Cook rushed for 157 yards, including a 74-yard touchdown, while the Bills generated +0.297 offensive EPA per play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="79">The defense also improved, although Geno Smith&#x27;s ankle injury and the appearance of Cade Klubnik for much of the second half belong in any evaluation of Buffalo&#x27;s defensive performance.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="80">DEPI also dropped Buffalo two places, to seventh at +4.45.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="81">At 8-1, Buffalo has little reason to panic. But its underlying numbers suggest a somewhat less dominant team than San Francisco, the Rams and several other contenders.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="82"><strong>10. Tampa Bay Buccaneers — 57.68</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="83"><strong>3-6 | Previous rank: 4 | DEPI: 11</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="84">Tampa Bay&#x27;s six-place fall is the most dramatic movement in this week&#x27;s published Efficiency rankings.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="85">It is also one of the least informative.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="86">The Buccaneers were on a bye, and their score declined just 0.04 points. The more meaningful issue remains the enormous difference between Tampa Bay&#x27;s defense and offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="87">DEPI credits the defense with +2.18 points but assigns the offense -1.78.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="88">That imbalance helps explain why a team that grades surprisingly well in Efficiency has managed only three victories.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="89">Tampa Bay heads to Detroit next week for an interesting test of whether its defense can keep the Buccaneers competitive against another improving offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="91">Ranking movement is useful, but improvement in the underlying score often tells us more.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="92">The Rams, Lions and Bengals produced the three largest Efficiency gains when comparing Week 9 and Week 10 using consistent data.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="94">These aren&#x27;t simply teams benefiting from favorable outcomes or the movement of competitors.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="95">All three generated strong offensive performances while improving the defensive side of their statistical profiles.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="96">The Rams provide the most convincing example. Their recent performance increasingly resembles the team DEPI had been projecting before the results caught up.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="97">Cincinnati is somewhat different. The Bengals already possessed one of football&#x27;s most dangerous passing attacks, but the defense was holding back their overall projection. The improved defensive results are helping close that gap.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="98">Detroit is following a similar pattern, although the quarterback injury to New England complicates how much credit the Lions should receive for Sunday&#x27;s defensive dominance.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="99">Cleveland and Indianapolis also made meaningful moves, each climbing four places in the published Efficiency rankings.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="100">The Browns recovered from an early 17-0 deficit to defeat Houston 27-24, while Indianapolis ended a five-game losing streak with a 24-22 victory over Miami.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="101">Both improved their underlying scores, but neither has yet established a particularly convincing forward-looking profile. The Colts rank 19th in DEPI, with Cleveland 25th.</p>

<h2 id="fallers" data-source-paragraph="102"><strong>Biggest Fallers: Pittsburgh and New England Take a Beating</strong></h2>

<p data-source-paragraph="103">The most significant negative performances came from Pittsburgh and New England.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="104">Pittsburgh&#x27;s Efficiency rating declined <strong>4.70 points</strong>, while New England lost <strong>4.60</strong>. Those were comfortably the largest decreases of Week 10.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="105">The Steelers&#x27; problems were especially troubling because Cincinnati exposed them in the exact area that matters most against elite AFC opponents.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="106">Burrow and Chase repeatedly attacked Pittsburgh&#x27;s pass defense, generating +0.368 offensive EPA per play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="107">The Steelers couldn&#x27;t disrupt the passing game consistently enough, and their own offense couldn&#x27;t keep pace until the outcome was effectively decided.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="108">Pittsburgh also suffered the largest DEPI decline, losing 1.38 projected points and falling five positions from 12th to 17th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="109">That is a substantial one-week reassessment.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="110">New England fell from eighth to tenth in DEPI after its disastrous afternoon against Detroit. They fall all the way to 25th if we factor in Tommy DeVito as the QB instead of Drake Maye.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="111">The Patriots&#x27; offensive performance was especially poor, producing -0.264 EPA per play. Maye&#x27;s early departure makes it difficult to separate the team&#x27;s underlying offensive limitations from the immediate quarterback problem.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="112">But the result still counts in Efficiency, which measures what happened rather than what might have happened with healthier personnel.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="113">The distinction matters.</p>

<h2 id="ravens" data-source-paragraph="127"><strong>The Ravens: Five Straight Losses, but DEPI Actually Improves</strong></h2>

<p data-source-paragraph="128">The Ravens lost 23-20 to the previously winless Chargers on Monday night, falling to 4-6 and extending their losing streak to five games.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="129">Somehow, their Power Index ranking went up.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="130">Baltimore remains 17th in Efficiency with a rating of 45.20, a decline of 1.45 points using consistent inputs.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="131">That reflects another disappointing performance from a team that began the season expecting to compete for a championship.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="132">The Chargers entered Monday averaging just 15.1 points per game, the lowest figure in football. They scored 23 against the Ravens, rushed for 160 yards and controlled enough of the game to secure their first victory.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="133">The Ravens, meanwhile, managed a ridiculous 16 rushing yards on 15 attempts. Derrick Henry finished with 10 yards on 10 carries.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="134">Jacoby Brissett&#x27;s first Baltimore start was considerably more encouraging than that rushing performance.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="135">Brissett completed 33 of 49 passes for 340 yards. Mark Andrews caught eight passes for 82 yards, and six Ravens receivers finished with at least 30 receiving yards.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="136">Baltimore found ways to move the football through the air, even though the offense ultimately generated a slightly negative -0.029 EPA per play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="137">The passing production wasn&#x27;t enough to overcome an almost nonexistent running game, another uneven defensive performance and Zay Flowers&#x27; late fumble that ended Baltimore&#x27;s final opportunity to tie or win.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="138">So how did DEPI improve?</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="180"><strong>Indianapolis at Houston: The Biggest Difference Has an Obvious Explanation</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="181">The model&#x27;s largest disagreement is an extraordinary 11.02 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="182">DEPI essentially considers Indianapolis and Houston equally strong. The Colts rate -1.12, while Houston sits at -1.17. Home-field advantage is almost entirely responsible for the model&#x27;s Houston -1.48 projection.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="183">The market has Houston favored by 12.5.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="184">Why such a massive difference?</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="185">Indianapolis will be missing both starting offensive tackles, Bernhard Raimann and Jalen Travis, along with cornerback Charvarius Ward, tight end Will Mallory and defensive tackle Jerry Tillery. Cornerback Sauce Gardner and reserve tackle Charlie Heck are also questionable.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="186">That&#x27;s not the sort of injury situation a basic quarterback-adjusted model can handle particularly well.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="187">Losing both starting tackles creates problems that can compound rather than simply add together. Protection assignments change, weaker replacements may be isolated against quality pass rushers, and the offense may have to sacrifice receiving options to provide additional blocking help.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="188">The defensive injuries create similar concerns in coverage.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="189">The market is presumably accounting for at least some of that risk, although the available evidence doesn&#x27;t allow us to determine exactly how much of the spread reflects injuries.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="190">The model&#x27;s disagreement remains worth investigating, but <strong>Indianapolis +12.5 is not automatically an 11-point edge</strong>.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="191">Sometimes a large discrepancy identifies an opportunity.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="192">Sometimes it identifies something your model is missing.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="193">This is a particularly good candidate for the second explanation.</p>
