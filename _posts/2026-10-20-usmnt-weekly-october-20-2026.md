<p data-draft-paragraph="2">The international break is over, and several Americans returned to their clubs with some of their best performances of the season.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="3">Gio Reyna scored against Paris Saint-Germain and delivered one of his most complete performances since joining Strasbourg. Tim Weah had two assists and was one of Marseille&#x27;s best players in a 5-2 win over Angers.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="4">Johnny Cardoso is becoming an increasingly important piece of Atlético Madrid&#x27;s midfield, playing an excellent 90 minutes in a Champions League victory over Manchester United. Yunus Musah also delivered an encouraging performance for AC Milan, showing how valuable his athleticism can be when he is deployed in the right midfield role.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="5">Meanwhile, 18-year-old Peyton Miller continues to make his case as one of the most exciting young players in the American pool. He provided two assists in New England&#x27;s midweek win over Cincinnati, then helped create the winner against Chicago as the Revolution secured a pair of important victories in their playoff push.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="6">There were other encouraging developments across Europe. Malik Tillman scored in the Europa League, Tanner Tessmann scored the winning goal in his first Ligue 1 start of the season, and Alex Freeman continued establishing himself in Villarreal&#x27;s rotation.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="7">Not everything went smoothly. Chris Richards endured a miserable week with Crystal Palace, Weston McKennie was part of a lopsided Juventus defeat, and PSV lost twice with Sergiño Dest in the lineup. Several Americans also remain sidelined by injuries.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="8">Still, the major takeaway is positive. For several years, much of the USMNT discussion revolved around talented players struggling for consistent opportunities at their clubs. This week provided more evidence of Americans earning meaningful roles and, in some cases, becoming important players for good European teams.</p>

<h2 id="spotlight-players" data-draft-paragraph="22">Spotlight Players</h2>

<h3 id="gio-reyna-strasbourg" data-draft-paragraph="23"><span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/gio-reyna.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy">Gio Reyna, Strasbourg</span></h3>

<p data-draft-paragraph="24">This was the type of performance Reyna needed to remind everyone why he was once considered the most exciting young American player in Europe.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="25">Reyna scored Strasbourg&#x27;s opening goal in the 17th minute against Paris Saint-Germain, helping his team carry a surprising 1-0 lead into halftime before the defending French champions eventually came back to win 3-1.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="26">The goal was a nice piece of individual play. Gessime Yassine found Reyna on the right side of the penalty area, where the American worked the ball onto his left foot and drove a low shot toward the far corner. It took a slight deflection, but the initial movement and finish were excellent.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="29">There was some good defensive work as well, with Reyna contributing to Strasbourg&#x27;s efforts to frustrate PSG&#x27;s buildup during the first half.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="30">He was arguably Strasbourg&#x27;s most influential attacking player over the opening hour, helping them remain dangerous rather than simply dropping deep to defend their early lead.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="31">Alsasports compiled the player evaluations from three publications covering Strasbourg: Direct Racing, L&#x27;Équipe and Dernières Nouvelles d&#x27;Alsace. All three gave Reyna a 6/10, tying him for the highest consensus rating among Strasbourg&#x27;s starters. That was a strong evaluation in a defeat, with the starting lineup averaging only 4.8 across the three outlets.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="32">More importantly, the coverage specifically praised Reyna&#x27;s ability to direct the attack for much of the match, rather than simply rewarding him for scoring the opening goal.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="33">Eventually, PSG&#x27;s superior depth took over. Luis Enrique introduced several of his most dangerous players in the second half, including Ousmane Dembélé, who helped create the equalizer before scoring the winner in the 85th minute. Désiré Doué added another in stoppage time.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="34">The defeat was disappointing, but Reyna&#x27;s performance deserves recognition. He played the full 90 minutes and received some of the strongest evaluations among Strasbourg&#x27;s players.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="35">The bigger story is what has been happening over the past two months.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="36">Reyna has now put together eight consecutive appearances across his club and national-team appearances. That is an enormous development for a player whose previous five seasons were repeatedly interrupted by injuries and inconsistent opportunities.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="37">There is still reason for caution. His team has lost three consecutive league games, and not every performance during this stretch has been particularly impressive. Being healthy and starting regularly is only the first step.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="38">But against PSG, Reyna looked like more than a talented player capable of an occasional moment of brilliance. He looked like someone increasingly comfortable taking responsibility for his team&#x27;s attack.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="39">For the USMNT, that is considerably more valuable.</p>

<h3 id="johnny-cardoso-atletico-madrid" data-draft-paragraph="40"><span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/johnny-cardoso.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy">Johnny Cardoso, Atlético Madrid</span></h3>

<p data-draft-paragraph="41">Johnny Cardoso&#x27;s emergence at Atlético Madrid continues to be one of the most encouraging developments in the American player pool.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="42">After an injury-plagued first season in Spain, Cardoso has finally established himself as an important part of Diego Simeone&#x27;s midfield. Tuesday&#x27;s 2-0 Champions League victory over Manchester United was arguably his best evidence yet.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="43">Cardoso started at the base of Atlético&#x27;s three-man midfield, providing the defensive foundation that allowed Pablo Barrios and the other midfielders to operate more aggressively.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="44">The partnership with Barrios was especially effective.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="45">Cardoso consistently made himself available as an outlet when Atlético attempted to play through United&#x27;s pressure. Instead of remaining stationary in front of the center backs, he moved into open passing lanes, received under pressure and helped advance possession.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="46">When Barrios moved forward, Cardoso was generally responsible for maintaining the midfield structure and protecting the space behind him.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="47">This was an important part of Atlético&#x27;s control over the game. United frequently struggled to progress through the middle, while Cardoso helped prevent turnovers from becoming dangerous counterattacks.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="54">The larger development is that Cardoso is no longer simply trying to regain his place after injuries derailed his first Atlético season. He is beginning to demonstrate that he can be a foundational part of Simeone&#x27;s midfield.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="55">There is still competition from Morten Hjulmand and veteran Koke, so his starting position is not guaranteed indefinitely.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="56">But Cardoso offers an intriguing combination of qualities.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="57">If he can remain healthy and continue playing this well against Champions League opposition, he could force himself into the center of Mauricio Pochettino&#x27;s plans for 2030.</p>

<h3 id="peyton-miller-new-england-revolution" data-draft-paragraph="58"><span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/peyton-miller.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy">Peyton Miller, New England Revolution</span></h3>

<p data-draft-paragraph="59">Peyton Miller is making it increasingly difficult to determine whether his long-term future is actually at left back.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="60">The 18-year-old delivered another outstanding week for New England, playing 178 minutes across two victories and contributing to three goals as the Revolution secured a playoff berth and strengthened their position in the Eastern Conference.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="61">He started Wednesday&#x27;s 3-2 victory at FC Cincinnati in an advanced attacking role and provided assists on both of Dor Turgeman&#x27;s goals.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="62">The two assists demonstrated different parts of his rapidly developing attacking game.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="63">In the 21st minute, Joshua Wynder found Miller with a long diagonal pass toward the right side. Miller controlled the ball and delivered an accurate pass into the penalty area for Turgeman to finish first-time.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="66">It was an excellent example of how Miller&#x27;s attacking game has progressed.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="67">His speed has always been extraordinary, but he is becoming more effective at recognizing when to use it, drawing defenders toward him and finding teammates in dangerous positions.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="68">Miller was also dangerous throughout the Chicago game, producing three shots, two on target, and showing the same willingness to attack defenders.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="69">Perhaps the most impressive improvement is how many different ways Miller can now hurt an opponent. His acceleration is remarkable, but his game has become much more than a series of sprints down the sideline.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="70">He recognizes opportunities to arrive at the back post, times his runs to stay onside and is increasingly comfortable attacking defenders one-on-one. His passing has also become more sophisticated, particularly when he moves inside instead of simply delivering crosses from wide positions.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="71">Miller now has seven goals and seven MLS-credited assists this season, extraordinary production for an 18-year-old who spent much of his development playing as a defender.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="72">The position question is becoming fascinating.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="73">For the USMNT, Miller initially appeared to be a potential long-term answer at left back, a position where Antonee Robinson has been remarkably dependable but will be 32 by the 2030 World Cup.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="74">Miller&#x27;s athleticism and attacking ability could eventually make him an outstanding modern fullback. However, playing higher up the field is allowing him to develop more of the technical skills and decision-making required to become a complete attacker.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="75">He still has defensive work to do, particularly when defending closer to his own goal. His initial USMNT experience demonstrated that there is a significant difference between attacking MLS defenders and handling international-level defensive assignments.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="76">But at this point, it would be premature to insist his future must be at fullback.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="77">If he continues developing his passing, dribbling and attacking instincts, he could become too dangerous to move away from the front line.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="78">Either way, Miller looks increasingly like a player who could have a significant role with the USMNT over the next cycle.</p>

<h3 id="tim-weah-marseille" data-draft-paragraph="79"><span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/tim-weah.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy">Tim Weah, Marseille</span></h3>

<p data-draft-paragraph="80">Tim Weah&#x27;s season at Marseille has been a bit of a roller coaster.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="81">He has established himself as a regular starter and even earned the captain&#x27;s armband, but some early defensive struggles and a red card against PSG raised legitimate questions about his performances.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="82">This week was much more encouraging as Weah played 90 minutes in Thursday&#x27;s disappointing 1-0 Europa League defeat to Olympiacos, then followed it with an excellent performance in Marseille&#x27;s 5-2 victory at Angers on Sunday.</p>
