Gio Reyna, Cole Campbell, Sergiño Dest and Yunus Musah supplied the strongest American performances of the week. Reyna's first Strasbourg start carried the kind of attacking influence he has spent years trying to turn into a regular club role. Campbell scored in Elversberg's stunning Bundesliga-opening win over Bayer Leverkusen. Dest added another goal and assist for PSV, while Musah again looked increasingly comfortable in AC Milan's midfield.

There were rougher developments, too. Chris Richards had a difficult individual assignment against Erling Haaland, Brenden Aaronson was withdrawn at halftime after an ineffective start for Leeds, Tanner Tessmann struggled in Lyon's Champions League elimination, and Utrecht confirmed that Kevin Paredes will be out for several months with another ankle injury.

**Up:** Reyna's first Strasbourg start was the sort of performance he badly needed after years of inconsistent club minutes. Campbell scored nine minutes into Elversberg's first Bundesliga game and looked comfortable at the level. Dest has two goals and two assists through PSV's first three league matches, while Musah has started both of Milan's opening games and earned another positive local review.

**Encouraging role developments:** Balogun's move to Everton, Freeman's first meaningful La Liga cameo, Gozo's 22-minute Premier League debut and Banks' consecutive full Bundesliga starts all point in the right direction.

**The concern group:** Aaronson's 45-minute outing against Brentford was a poor follow-up to a lukewarm opening-weekend review. Scally is openly on Gladbach's transfer list, Reynolds has barely played since joining Rennes, Wright remains out with a serious quad injury, and Paredes faces another lengthy rehabilitation.

This was an extremely encouraging first start for Reyna. He played 76 minutes as Strasbourg came from behind to beat Lens 2-1, and while he did not record a goal or assist, he was involved in a steady stream of the side's most dangerous sequences.

He was active almost immediately: a blocked header in the second minute, a neat left-sided combination and carry into the area five minutes later, then a difficult left-footed volley in the 34th that forced Robin Risser into an excellent save. Before halftime he also found room on the left to place a cross onto a teammate's head.

What stood out was the variety. Reyna did not stay central and wait for the ball. He drifted left to combine, received between midfield and defense, carried toward the box and kept making himself available around the penalty area. *Maxifoot* gave him a 6.5, praising his freedom of movement, ability to eliminate defenders and availability between the lines.

He also helped connect the buildup to Gessime Yassine's equalizer early in the second half, then participated in another sequence that ended in a goal erased by VAR for offside. His influence faded as the half went on, and Strasbourg became more dangerous again after his 76th-minute substitution. That is the caveat. But for a first start, this was about as promising as Reyna could have hoped for without getting on the scoresheet.

The assignment now is simple: make this a normal 70- or 80-minute role rather than an isolated glimpse of the player he can be.

Richards had a difficult night against Manchester City, particularly when matched with Erling Haaland. On the first goal, he lost track of Haaland as Antoine Semenyo's cross arrived. On City's fourth, Phil Foden broke the defensive structure and found Haaland in the box with Richards the closest defender; he did not close quickly enough to prevent the finish.

There is context. Palace was stretched by that stage, Foden had already broken the initial shape, and defending Haaland on a well-delivered cross is about as demanding an assignment as a center back can receive. Richards still will not enjoy watching either moment.

This was not 90 minutes of poor defending. Palace-focused ratings generally placed him around the middle of the team rather than treating him as the primary reason for the 4-1 loss, and Palace created enough to keep the match competitive for stretches. The bigger positive remains his role: he continues to start against opponents like City and is an established Premier League center back. If he is going to be the unquestioned leader of the next USMNT defense, though, these are the individual moments he needs to win more often.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/cole-campbell.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Cole Campbell, Elversberg (Bundesliga)</span>

Campbell could hardly have asked for a better opening to Elversberg's Bundesliga season. Starting on the left of the attack in the club's first-ever Bundesliga game, he scored in the ninth minute to put Elversberg 2-0 ahead against Malik Tillman's Bayer Leverkusen.

The goal came from alertness as much as technique. Leverkusen played a poor pass in its own half, Campbell reacted first to collect the loose ball and drove his shot home before the visitors could reset. Elversberg later survived a late push to win 3-2, an enormous result for a club that was in Germany's fourth tier only a few years ago.

Regional outlet SR described Elversberg as fearless from the opening whistle. Campbell fit that approach well: he played about 65 minutes, put all three shots on target, won eight duels and repeatedly threatened with his speed from the left. This was his first Bundesliga start for Elversberg and easily one of the most substantial senior performances of his career.

His upside has been obvious for years. Elversberg gives him the chance to collect the senior evidence that never arrived consistently at Dortmund. Scoring against Leverkusen is a pretty good way to begin.

Musah followed his strong opening-weekend performance with another full 90 in Milan's 2-0 win over Venezia. The local reviews were more mixed this time, but the overall description was encouraging: efficient passing, huge defensive range and the ability to turn recoveries into immediate attacks.

*La Gazzetta dello Sport* gave him a 6.5 and called him Milan's best midfielder, praising aggressive early runs before he settled into the functional work of winning the ball and moving it quickly. *SportMediaset* also awarded a 6.5, emphasizing that Milan often became dangerous soon after Musah recovered possession. Casa Milan and Sky were closer to 6, noting some inaccurate passing.

The clear negative came in the 54th minute, when a loose ball fell to him with most of the goal open and he hit the crossbar. Several ratings marked him down specifically for the miss. It keeps the match from being another standout performance, but the larger trend is good. Milan is asking Musah to do more connective, defensive and decision-making work rather than simply carry the ball out of pressure. Two starts and two credible performances is an encouraging opening answer.

Sullivan's 50th-minute goal in Philadelphia's 3-1 win over Red Bull New York was spectacular. Danley Jean Jacques' long diagonal found him on the right; Sullivan's first touch brought the ball down and put him in position to attack the defender in one action, then he cut onto his left foot and ripped a low finish into the far corner.

The *Philadelphia Inquirer* called it easily the best of his six first-team goals, and Union interim manager Ryan Richter described it as Sullivan's first truly special individual scoring play at senior level. It extended his run to four straight MLS matches with a goal or assist, making him the first 16-year-old to do it.

The hype has existed for years. The difference now is that there is senior production to support it.

In the 65th minute, after Milan Iloski's free kick was flicked across the box, Pierre arrived at the back side to slide forward and poke home Philadelphia's third.

He now has four goals and an assist during the Union's seven-match unbeaten run, absurd attacking production for a center back even if much of it comes from set pieces. The goals are a bonus. The more significant development is that he is beginning to match unusual physical tools with more consistent decisions: at 6-foot-5, he can dominate aerially, but his ability to cover ground and defend outside his box gives him the chance to become more than a traditional penalty-area center back.

## Premier League

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/tyler-adams.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Tyler Adams, Bournemouth</span>

Adams again began on the bench for Bournemouth and entered in the second half of the 1-1 draw with Everton. He replaced Lewis Cook around the 69th minute with Bournemouth protecting a lead before Everton equalized from a stoppage-time corner. There was not much opportunity to judge his individual performance, but two league matches without a start makes the early depth-chart question worth monitoring.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/antonee-robinson.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Antonee Robinson, Fulham</span>

Robinson started both Fulham league matches: 82 minutes in Monday's 3-2 loss to Chelsea and the full 90 in Sunday's 1-0 defeat at Sunderland. Álvaro Arbeloa's more aggressive possession structure asks the fullbacks to receive and progress in crowded areas rather than simply overlap. It is too early to judge the fit, but Fulham has started with consecutive losses and Robinson has not yet had his usual final-third influence.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/brenden-aaronson.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Brenden Aaronson, Leeds</span>

Aaronson started the 1-1 draw with Brentford but lasted only 45 minutes. LeedsUnited.news gave him a 4/10 and said the match simply passed him by; Harry Wilson replaced him at halftime as Daniel Farke changed the midfield structure. It is a disappointing follow-up to a lukewarm opening-weekend review. Aaronson remains in the rotation, but he needs to turn valued pressing and movement into more consistent attacking production.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/haji-wright.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Haji Wright, Coventry</span>

Wright remains sidelined by the serious quadriceps injury expected to keep him out for roughly three months. The frustrating part is not simply the absence; it removes his immediate chance to test whether his Championship scoring production can translate to the Premier League.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/zavier-gozo.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Zavier Gozo, Crystal Palace</span>

Gozo made his Premier League debut against Manchester City, replacing Yéremy Pino in the 68th minute. The match was already getting away from Palace, limiting his chance to make an impact, but 22 Premier League minutes only ten days after arriving from Real Salt Lake is an encouraging early signal. The first target is simply to keep making the matchday squad.

## La Liga

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/johnny-cardoso.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Johnny Cardoso, Atlético Madrid</span>

Cardoso made his first La Liga appearance of the season, replacing Morten Hjulmand in the 85th minute of Atlético's 3-1 win at Sevilla. Six minutes is not enough to evaluate, but it is progress after two unused-bench appearances. The depth-chart challenge remains: Hjulmand, Pablo Barrios and the other midfield options are functioning without him, so he is trying to break into an established group rather than reclaim an automatic role.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/alex-freeman.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Alex Freeman, Villarreal</span>

Freeman received his most substantial minutes of the season in Villarreal's 1-0 loss at Alavés, replacing Santiago Mouriño at right back in the 62nd minute. There was no major attacking contribution, but the 28-plus minutes matter after a token opening appearance and a bench spot against Atlético. The next step is turning these cameos into real competition for starts.

## Serie A

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/christian-pulisic.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Christian Pulisic, AC Milan</span>

Pulisic was available but remained an unused substitute in Milan's 2-0 win over Venezia. His delayed post-World Cup return has allowed the club to bring him along slowly, so there is no serious reason for concern yet. But after two league matches without an appearance, the next several weeks should clarify how Rúben Amorim plans to use him in the reshaped attack.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/weston-mckennie.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Weston McKennie, Juventus</span>

McKennie missed Juventus' 2-0 victory over Parma because of fatigue. Luciano Spalletti confirmed the absence before the match, making this workload management rather than a tactical omission after he started the opener.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/benjamin-cremaschi.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Benjamin Cremaschi, Parma</span>

Cremaschi played roughly the final half hour of Parma's 2-0 loss to Juventus, continuing his return after injuries disrupted most of last season. He did not change an attack that spent much of the match defending deep and struggling to create, but the minutes matter more than the performance right now. Establishing himself as a regular substitute is the first step toward something larger.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/gianluca-busio.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Gianluca Busio, Venezia</span>

Busio played another full 90 as Venezia lost 2-0 to Milan. Sky Italia's 6 placed him around the middle of Venezia's ratings rather than among the players blamed for the defeat. Venezia has lost its first two matches, but Busio continues to start centrally and carry significant responsibility in possession, which remains a useful club situation even if the team faces a difficult season.

## Germany

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/malik-tillman.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Malik Tillman, Bayer Leverkusen</span>

Tillman started against Campbell's Elversberg but had little influence as Leverkusen somehow fell behind 3-0. He played about 65 minutes before being substituted during the attempted rescue. The entire attack deserves criticism: Leverkusen controlled possession but created too little until the match was already slipping away. Tillman's World Cup and cup-opener form remain more meaningful than one poor league afternoon, but this was a step backward.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/noahkai-banks.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Noahkai Banks, Augsburg</span>

Banks played 90 minutes in Augsburg's 3-0 win over Schalke and helped open the Bundesliga season with a clean sheet. Combined with his complete DFB-Pokal match the previous weekend, he has started with consecutive 90s. For a 19-year-old center back, being dependable enough to remain on the field every week is more useful than a string of highlights. If he ultimately chooses the United States, regular Bundesliga minutes will make him difficult to leave outside the center-back conversation.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/joe-scally.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Joe Scally, Borussia Mönchengladbach</span>

Scally's situation is clear. Gladbach sporting director Rouven Schröder publicly placed both Scally and Florian Neuhaus on the transfer list after manager Eugen Polanski excluded them from the cup squad. At 23, with substantial Bundesliga and USMNT experience, finding a club where he can play is plainly the best outcome.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/mathis-albert.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Mathis Albert, Borussia Dortmund</span>

Albert did not feature for Dortmund's senior side, but he dropped to the youth/U21 group and promptly produced a goal and an assist in a 4-1 Premier League International Cup win over Fulham's U21s. He created Dortmund's equalizer with a long pass in the 25th minute, then scored three minutes later after pouncing on a poor Fulham pass. Dortmund's official report described him as lively throughout. The developmental question is no longer youth-level talent; it is translating those chances into Bundesliga minutes.

### Lennard Maloney, Mainz

Maloney was an unused substitute for the second straight competitive match as Mainz drew 0-0 with Paderborn. Mainz used several substitutions without turning to him. It is still early, but the pattern is becoming difficult to ignore: he currently appears to be depth rather than a major piece of the midfield rotation.

## Ligue 1

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/folarin-balogun.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Folarin Balogun, Monaco / Everton</span>

Balogun did not play for Monaco, but the reason is now clear: Everton and Monaco agreed a deal to send the USMNT striker back to the Premier League. Reports placed the fee at €45 million plus €5 million in potential bonuses and a sell-on percentage, with the medical and formalities expected before the deadline.

Everton's expected departure of Beto to Fiorentina should give Balogun a genuine chance to lead the line rather than merely provide depth. He returns to England as a different player from the one who could not break through at Arsenal, having scored 18 goals across Ligue 1 and the Champions League last season before adding three for the United States at the World Cup. The move raises the weekly competition and the risk if he does not perform, but it offers a clear opportunity at a higher level.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/tim-weah.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Tim Weah, Marseille</span>

Weah again started at right back and wore the captain's armband in Marseille's 2-0 loss at Monaco. Marseille-focused ratings criticized his lack of attacking influence as Monaco controlled the match. There remains a useful distinction between role and output: captaining Marseille is meaningful evidence of the staff's trust, but he has not yet made that trust translate into especially influential performances.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/tanner-tessmann.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Tanner Tessmann, Lyon</span>

Tessmann started Lyon's decisive Champions League qualifier against Fenerbahçe and played about 75 minutes in the 2-1 loss that eliminated the club. Lyon struggled to control midfield for long stretches, and Tessmann could not consistently move possession through Fenerbahçe's pressure. Three days later he was an unused substitute in a 1-1 league draw with Le Havre. Reports of Lyon's financial pressure make a transfer possible, though the weekend bench could also have been simple rest.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/mark-mckenzie.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Mark McKenzie, Toulouse</span>

McKenzie played another full 90 as Toulouse drew Brest 2-2 and has now played every minute of the club's first two Ligue 1 matches. Toulouse will hate the 95th-minute equalizer after twice taking the lead, but McKenzie's starting role appears secure. Consistent Ligue 1 starts give him a real chance to push back toward the USMNT rotation.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/bryan-reynolds.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Bryan Reynolds, Rennes</span>

Reynolds was an unused substitute in Rennes' 3-2 win over Le Mans and has played only two Ligue 1 minutes through the club's first two league matches. The move to Rennes was supposed to test him at a stronger level; it only helps if he gets onto the field. This is an early concern.

## Eredivisie

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/sergino-dest.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Sergiño Dest, PSV</span>

Dest played all 90 minutes and registered a goal and an assist as PSV demolished Utrecht 6-1. The assist came on Guus Til's first-half goal; Dest capped the scoring himself in stoppage time after another aggressive attacking run. He finished with four shots, three on target and six crosses.

Through three Eredivisie appearances, Dest has two goals and two assists. Few American roles translate so directly to what the USMNT can use: with PSV on the ball, he is essentially another attacker, able to overlap outside, dribble centrally or arrive late around the box. Healthy and this confident, he remains nearly impossible to replicate within the pool.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/ricardo-pepi.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Ricardo Pepi, PSV</span>

Pepi started his fourth consecutive Eredivisie match but was left out of PSV's scoring explosion. He played 77 minutes and had only one shot as six goals arrived around him. There is no need to overread one quiet afternoon: he already has two goals this season and, more importantly, appears to have established himself as PSV's first-choice striker rather than the high-efficiency backup he previously was.

### <span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/kevin-paredes.png' | relative_url }}" alt="">Kevin Paredes, Utrecht</span>

Utrecht confirmed that Paredes will miss several months with the ankle injury he suffered against Sparta Rotterdam. The club said he had been feeling increasingly comfortable as his minutes grew before the setback. That is what makes this especially frustrating: after two injury-hit seasons, the Utrecht move was supposed to provide a stable fresh start. Another rehabilitation process begins almost immediately.
