<p data-source-paragraph="2"><strong>November 24, 2026 | Through Week 11</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="3">The San Francisco 49ers are back on top.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="4">Their convincing 35-10 victory over Minnesota in Mexico City, combined with Jacksonville&#x27;s surprising loss to the Giants, has pushed the 49ers back into first place in the Dual Eights Efficiency Index. They also remain atop the Dual Eights Power Index (DEPI), although the Rams are less than a third of a point behind them.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="5">Kansas City continues to climb after a third consecutive convincing victory, while Cincinnati suffered the largest decline in Efficiency this week. Detroit has won six straight games and moved into third in DEPI, setting up an outstanding Thanksgiving matchup with Chicago.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="6">And despite having a losing record, the Dallas Cowboys are now a top-10 team according to our forward-looking model.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="9"><strong>1. San Francisco 49ers | 69.06</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="10"><strong>9-1 | Week 10: 2 | DEPI: 1 (+7.63)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="11">San Francisco&#x27;s offense is becoming quite the juggernaut.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="12">Brock Purdy threw for three touchdowns against a Minnesota defense that came in rated the best in the league. George Kittle caught two of them and Mike Evans returned to contribute six catches for 109 yards and a touchdown. The 49ers generated +0.329 EPA per offensive play</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="13">The defense contributed as well, holding Minnesota to -0.116 EPA per play, with Ji&#x27;Ayir Brown returning an interception for a touchdown and Deommodore Lenoir forcing a fumble.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="14">San Francisco gained 1.86 Efficiency points and 0.86 DEPI points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="15">The most interesting part of the 49ers&#x27; profile remains how much of their superiority comes from the offense. Their projected offensive contribution in DEPI is an outstanding +6.41 points, while the defense contributes just +0.37.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="16">This is not necessarily the most complete team in football. But with Purdy operating an offense that now features a healthy Evans alongside Kittle, the other phases don&#x27;t need to be exceptional for the 49ers to be awfully difficult to beat.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="17"><strong>2. Los Angeles Rams | 66.05</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="18"><strong>6-4 | Week 10: 3 | DEPI: 2 (+7.31)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="19">The Rams moved into second place without playing a game.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="20">Some of that improvement comes from revisions to the underlying data. Recalculating their Week 10 performance increased their previously published score from 63.24 to 65.24. The remaining 0.81-point improvement came from changes in opponent strength and the league-wide results during their bye.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="21">The Rams continue to have one of the most balanced profiles in DEPI, with their offense contributing +5.19 points and defense another +2.49. Special teams remain a weakness at -0.61.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="22">The difference between Los Angeles and San Francisco in DEPI is now just 0.32 points. There is then nearly a two-point gap between the Rams and third-place Detroit.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="23">Of course, the model&#x27;s assessment of the Rams&#x27; overall strength doesn&#x27;t necessarily reflect the version of the team that will take the field Wednesday night against Green Bay. The injuries are piling up offensively with Nacua missing and key losses on the offensive line.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="24">We&#x27;ll get into that when examining this week&#x27;s spreads.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="25"><strong>3. Jacksonville Jaguars | 65.09</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="26"><strong>7-3 | Week 10: 1 | DEPI: 7 (+3.59)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="27">Jacksonville&#x27;s 23-18 loss to the Giants was a disappointing result for a team that had spent much of the season establishing itself as one of the most complete in football.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="28">Trevor Lawrence threw for 301 yards, but he needed 47 attempts to get there and threw two horrendous interceptions. The Jaguars generated -0.082 EPA per offensive play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="29">The defense largely did its job, holding the Giants to 238 total yards, three second-half points and -0.141 EPA per play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="30">The offense and special teams were responsible for most of Jacksonville&#x27;s 2.27-point Efficiency decline. DEPI also reduced the Jaguars&#x27; rating by 1.18 points, with nearly a full point coming out of their projected offensive contribution.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="31">Jacksonville remains comfortably among the three most efficient teams this season, but the Power Index has become increasingly less enthusiastic about the Jaguars relative to some of the other contenders.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="32">They get an opportunity to get the offense back on track against Tennessee this weekend.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="33"><strong>4. Kansas City Chiefs | 62.08</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="34"><strong>8-2 | Week 10: 7 | DEPI: 8 (+3.07)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="35">It may be time for the Power Index to start taking Kansas City a little more seriously.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="36">The Chiefs beat Arizona 34-7, their third consecutive convincing victory, with improvements across all three phases reflected in the Efficiency Index.</p>

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-efficiency-week-eleven-2026/mahomes-kelce-cardinals.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Patrick Mahomes and Travis Kelce celebrate a touchdown against Arizona in Week 11" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

<p data-source-paragraph="37">Emmett Johnson generated 143 yards from scrimmage, Travis Kelce caught two touchdowns and the defense held Arizona to just 205 total yards.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="38">Kansas City produced +0.256 EPA per offensive play while allowing -0.127 defensively. Of course, Arizona is barely an NFL offense with Josh Johnson stepping in to quarterback off the street, but KC’s defense has been solid the past few weeks.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="39">The Chiefs gained 2.51 Efficiency points and 0.87 DEPI points, although that wasn&#x27;t enough to move them out of eighth in the Power Index.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="40">The biggest discrepancy remains their projected offensive strength. DEPI gives Kansas City an offensive contribution of +2.15 points, considerably less than the top offenses in the league. Their special teams contribution is also negative at -0.67.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="41">The Chiefs have performed like one of the four best teams in football, but the model continues to project them closer to the second tier of contenders.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="42">Thursday night in Buffalo should provide some useful evidence as to which assessment is closer to reality.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="43"><strong>5. Chicago Bears | 61.15</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="44"><strong>8-2 | Week 10: 4 | DEPI: 4 (+4.52)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="45">The Bears have now won five consecutive games, although their 21-13 victory over New Orleans wasn&#x27;t particularly impressive offensively.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="46">Chicago generated -0.104 EPA per offensive play. Both of its offensive touchdown drives began in Saints territory, with the defense producing six sacks, two takeaways and a safety.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="47">The Bears deserve credit for creating those opportunities and taking advantage of them. But the final score was more flattering to the offense than the underlying performance.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="48">The defensive and special teams improvements were enough to offset the offensive decline in Efficiency, where Chicago gained a modest 0.10 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="49">DEPI was less forgiving, reducing the Bears&#x27; overall rating by 0.87 points, including a 1.26-point reduction in their projected offensive contribution.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="50">Chicago remains fourth in DEPI and is clearly one of the better teams in the NFC. The question is whether its offense can consistently sustain drives when the defense isn&#x27;t handing it favorable field position.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="51">That&#x27;s going to be particularly important against Detroit on Thanksgiving.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="52"><strong>6. Seattle Seahawks | 60.19</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="53"><strong>6-4 | Week 10: 8 | DEPI: 9 (+2.60)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="54">Like the Rams, Seattle benefited from changes in the surrounding data and opponent strength during its bye.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="55">The Seahawks&#x27; profile remains largely unchanged. Their projected defensive contribution of +2.18 points is doing much of the work to compensate for an offense that DEPI rates slightly below average at -0.38.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="56">The more immediate question is whether Sam Darnold will return against San Francisco.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="57">Darnold returned to practice Monday and reportedly took a substantial number of reps, although Mike Macdonald indicated that a decision on his availability would come after Wednesday&#x27;s work.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="58">Our current projection assumes Darnold plays. Switching to Drew Lock reduces Seattle&#x27;s Power rating by 1.38 points with the remaining assumptions held constant.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="59">Considering the offense Seattle faces this weekend, it can&#x27;t afford to give away much more on its own offensive side of the ball.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="60"><strong>7. Detroit Lions | 60.05</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="61"><strong>8-2 | Week 10: 6 | DEPI: 3 (+5.35)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="62">Detroit has quietly put together six consecutive victories and now has the third-highest Power rating in football.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="63">The Lions beat Tampa Bay 31-21 behind another impressive offensive performance. Jared Goff threw for 279 yards and two touchdowns, while Jahmyr Gibbs scored twice in the fourth quarter.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="64">Detroit generated +0.287 EPA per offensive play, which was enough to overcome a less impressive defensive performance and produce a 1.35-point Efficiency improvement.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="65">Interestingly, the Lions fell one spot in Efficiency despite improving their score. They climbed two places in DEPI. Detroit&#x27;s projected offensive contribution is now +4.54 points, while its defense remains slightly below average at -0.23.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="66">There are still legitimate questions about whether the defense can hold up against the better offenses in the league, but the offense gives Detroit a chance to beat anybody.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="67">The Power Index is increasingly buying the Lions as a legitimate championship contender.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="68"><strong>8. Buffalo Bills | 58.10</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="69"><strong>9-1 | Week 10: 9 | DEPI: 5 (+4.17)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="70">Buffalo&#x27;s 26-9 victory over Miami boosted their defensive profile. However, I believe the Bills are somewhat of a paper-tiger. Their defense has put up solid numbers recently, but they haven’t played against a starting QB in months.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="71">Against Kyle McCord they didn&#x27;t allow a touchdown and held Miami to -0.271 EPA per play. The Bills gained 1.22 Efficiency points, with the defense responsible for most of the improvement. Interestingly, they moved up two places in DEPI despite their actual Power rating declining by 0.29 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="72">Their projected offensive contribution declined by 0.84 points, while the defense improved by 0.46. Buffalo&#x27;s overall defensive projection is now just above average at +0.05. The offense remains the foundation of the team at +3.11.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="73">The Bills are 9-1 and continue to win games while some of the other AFC contenders have stumbled. Thursday&#x27;s matchup with Kansas City provides a real test to see whether their defense has actually improved or just feasted on inept opposing quarterbacks.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="74"><strong>9. Cincinnati Bengals | 56.06</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="75"><strong>7-3 | Week 10: 5 | DEPI: 6 (+3.75)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="76">Cincinnati suffered the largest Efficiency decline of any team this week following its 22-20 loss in Washington.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="77">Joe Burrow threw for 300 yards and two touchdowns, but the Commanders did an excellent job limiting Cincinnati&#x27;s two most dangerous receivers. Ja&#x27;Marr Chase finished with just 31 receiving yards and Tee Higgins added 43.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="78">The Bengals generated only +0.038 EPA per offensive play, while their defense allowed +0.066. Neither number is disastrous in isolation, but the offensive performance fell well short of the standard Cincinnati had established during its five-game winning streak.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="79">The Bengals lost 4.22 Efficiency points and 1.52 DEPI points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="80">Most of the latter came from a 1.29-point reduction in their projected offensive contribution. Their defense remains below average at -0.85.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="81">One disappointing offensive performance doesn&#x27;t erase the previous five victories. Burrow, Chase and Higgins are still capable of making Cincinnati one of the most dangerous teams in the AFC.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="82">But the Bengals&#x27; defensive limitations mean they have considerably less margin for error when the offense has an ordinary afternoon.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="83"><strong>10. Denver Broncos | 54.36</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="84"><strong>8-2 | Week 10: 11 | DEPI: 11 (+1.18)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="85">Denver replaces Tampa Bay in the Efficiency top 10 following its 38-24 victory over Las Vegas.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="86">Rookie Jonah Coleman rushed for 111 yards and three touchdowns, but the most significant contributions to Denver&#x27;s Efficiency improvement came from its defense and special teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="87">The Broncos blocked two punts, and Ja&#x27;Quan McMillian added a late interception return for a touchdown. Those plays help explain why an offense that generated a relatively ordinary +0.062 EPA per play was part of a team that scored 38 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="88">Denver gained 2.47 Efficiency points and 0.57 DEPI points, leaving it just outside the Power Index top 10. The Broncos have been one of the more interesting teams to evaluate this season. Their 8-2 record is outstanding, but DEPI still sees them as only modestly better than an average NFL team.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="89">Their ability to continue creating advantages defensively and on special teams may determine whether they can outperform that projection in January.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="115"><strong>Green Bay at Los Angeles Rams: How much do the injuries matter?</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="116"><strong>DEPI: Rams -8.26 | Market: Rams -2.5 | Difference: 5.76 points</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="117">This is the largest discrepancy in favor of a favorite, and on the surface it looks particularly appealing.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="118">The Rams are the second-best team in DEPI at +7.31. Green Bay is rated +0.59.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="119">That is a substantial difference before even considering the Rams&#x27; home-field advantage.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="120">But the injury report makes this one much more complicated.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="121">Los Angeles has already been operating without Puka Nacua and Davante Adams. Now Steve Avila, Coleman Shelton and Terrance Ferguson have been ruled out for Wednesday&#x27;s game.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="122">Kobie Turner and Justin Dedich are expected back, which helps, while Green Bay has activated Micah Parsons and could have him available for his season debut.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="123">The possibility of Parsons attacking a depleted Rams offensive line is exactly the kind of personnel-specific matchup the base model isn&#x27;t designed to evaluate.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="124">We can use the dashboard to experiment with additional injury adjustments. Reducing the Rams&#x27; rating by two points, for example, would move the projected spread to Rams -6.26.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="125">That&#x27;s still substantially higher than the market&#x27;s -2.5, although determining whether two points is the appropriate adjustment requires more than simply counting injured players.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="126">It&#x27;s an intriguing discrepancy, but probably one of the most difficult on the board to evaluate properly.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="127"><strong>Tennessee at Jacksonville: A little too much respect for the Jaguars?</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="128"><strong>DEPI: Jaguars -9.72 | Market: Jaguars -14.5 | Difference: 4.78 points</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="129">Jacksonville should win this game comfortably.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="130">The disagreement is over how comfortably.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="131">The Jaguars rank seventh in DEPI at +3.59, while Tennessee ranks 30th at -4.59. There is no question which team the model believes is better.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="132">However, laying more than two touchdowns requires a considerably more convincing offensive performance than Jacksonville delivered against the Giants.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="133">Tennessee&#x27;s defense was just dismantled by Dallas, so there is certainly a reasonable path to Jacksonville winning by a substantial margin.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="134">The model simply isn&#x27;t convinced the difference between the teams justifies a 14.5-point spread.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="135"><strong>Baltimore at Houston: Does the model still believe in the Ravens?</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="136"><strong>DEPI: Texans -2.24 | Market: Texans -6.5 | Difference: 4.26 points</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="137">Apparently, DEPI has more faith in the Ravens than most of us do at this point.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="138">Houston has improved to +0.06 in DEPI, while Baltimore sits at -0.65. That leaves only 0.71 points separating the teams on a neutral field.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="139">The current projection assumes Brissett starts for the Ravens.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="140">For anyone curious about the impact of Lamar Jackson, switching to Jackson on the dashboard would improve Baltimore&#x27;s rating by approximately 3.09 points and actually make the Ravens a slight favorite with the other assumptions held constant.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="141">That is purely a hypothetical exercise, not an indication that Lamar is returning.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="142">The argument for Baltimore being more competitive than the market expects is that the offense has improved with Brissett, while Houston&#x27;s latest defensive performance came against a struggling Indianapolis offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="143">The argument against Baltimore is pretty much everything we&#x27;ve watched over the past six weeks.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="144"><strong>Atlanta at Minnesota: The Vikings&#x27; record continues to flatter them</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="145"><strong>DEPI: Vikings -3.11 | Market: Vikings -7 | Difference: 3.89 points</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="146">Minnesota&#x27;s 7-3 record remains considerably more impressive than its Power Index rating.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="147">The Vikings are 15th in DEPI at +0.46, while Atlanta is rated -1.11. Kirk Cousins is already the assumed starting quarterback for Minnesota.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="148">The concern throughout the season has been whether the Vikings can generate enough offense when their defense isn&#x27;t controlling the game.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="149">San Francisco provided a fairly emphatic answer last week.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="150">Atlanta has plenty of problems of its own, but the model doesn&#x27;t see enough separating these two teams to justify Minnesota being favored by a full touchdown.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="151"><strong>Seattle at San Francisco: Can Seattle generate enough offense?</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="152"><strong>DEPI: 49ers -6.56 | Market: 49ers -3 | Difference: 3.56 points</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="153">This matchup features the best combined Efficiency rankings of any game on the Week 12 schedule.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="154">San Francisco ranks first and Seattle sixth, but the Power Index sees a considerably larger gap between the teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="155">The 49ers&#x27; projected offense is the best in the league at +6.41 points. Seattle&#x27;s defense is strong enough to create problems, but its projected offensive contribution remains negative.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="156">And that already assumes Sam Darnold returns.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="157">If Drew Lock starts instead, the projected San Francisco margin increases to approximately 7.94 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="158">Seattle&#x27;s defense is capable of keeping this competitive. The question is how much it can reasonably be expected to accomplish against San Francisco without getting more help from its own offense.</p>
