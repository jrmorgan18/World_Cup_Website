<p data-source-paragraph="2"><strong>October 13, 2026 | Through Week 5</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="3">Five weeks into the NFL season, the San Francisco 49ers have emerged as the best team in football. At least according to both of our models.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="4">San Francisco’s 32-29 victory in Seattle pushed the undefeated 49ers ahead of Jacksonville in the Dual Eights Efficiency Index, while their offensive dominance has opened a more substantial lead in our new forward-looking Power Index.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="5">The Jaguars remain close behind in Efficiency, Buffalo is surging, and the Ravens have moved back into the top five despite playing without Lamar Jackson.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="6">Elsewhere, the Patriots and Buccaneers have made significant improvements. Minnesota continues to defy conventional logic, reaching 5-0 behind the league’s best defense and special teams despite having its least efficient offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="7">And this week, we’re introducing something new.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="8">The <strong>Dual Eights Power Index (DEPI)</strong> joins our existing Efficiency rankings, giving us a second way to evaluate the league. Rather than simply measuring how teams have performed, we can begin estimating how strong they should be in future games, comparing matchups and identifying differences between our projections and the betting market.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="9">The two models won’t always agree. That’s part of the point.</p>

<h2 data-source-paragraph="10"><strong>Introducing the Dual Eights Power Index</strong></h2>

<p data-source-paragraph="11">The Dual Eights Efficiency Index measures how well a team has actually played this season. The new <strong>Dual Eights Power Index (DEPI)</strong> asks a different question: how strong should that team be going forward?</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="12">DEPI combines opponent-adjusted passing and rushing efficiency, success rates, special teams, historical performance and the expected starting quarterback. Unlike the Efficiency Index’s 0-100 scale, the Power Index expresses team strength in points above or below an average NFL team on a neutral field.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="13">A team rated +5 is projected to be approximately five points better than average. Comparing two teams’ ratings, then applying the model’s home-field adjustment, produces an estimated scoring margin for a matchup.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="14">The important distinction is that Efficiency describes what has happened, while DEPI attempts to project what happens next. The Power Index uses historical information and gives greater weight to recent games while accounting for changes at quarterback.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="15">The Power Index is still a beta model, and its limitations matter. Injuries beyond quarterback, weather, rest and specific personnel matchups are not explicitly modeled. These are estimates, not guarantees, and a disagreement with a sportsbook is not automatically a profitable betting opportunity.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="16">Both indexes are available on our<a href="https://dualeights.com/nfl/rankings/"> </a><a href="https://dualeights.com/nfl/rankings/">live NFL rankings dashboard</a>.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/sf.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="19"><strong>1. San Francisco 49ers — 68.7</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary"><span data-source-paragraph="20"><strong>5-0 | Week 4: 2 | Offense: 1 | Defense: 12 | Special teams: 20</strong></span> <span class="efficiency-team-depi">| DEPI: +9.86 (No. 1)</span></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="21">The 49ers have finally overtaken Jacksonville, and their case for being the league’s best team is increasingly difficult to dispute.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="22">Their 32-29 win at Seattle was particularly revealing because they faced one of the NFL’s best defensive units and still found enough offense to win.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="23">San Francisco’s first-ranked adjusted offense continues to drive its success. The defense is a more ordinary 12th and special teams rank 20th, so this is not a team dominating every phase of football.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="24">But when an offense is consistently better than everyone else’s, the other units don’t necessarily need to be exceptional.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="25">The Power Index is even more impressed, placing the 49ers first at +9.86, more than 2.5 points clear of second-place Buffalo.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/jax.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="26"><strong>2. Jacksonville Jaguars — 68.1</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary"><span data-source-paragraph="27"><strong>4-1 | Week 4: 1 | Offense: 4 | Defense: 4 | Special teams: 19</strong></span> <span class="efficiency-team-depi">| DEPI: +6.15 (No. 3)</span></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="28">Jacksonville fell out of first place despite beating Philadelphia 19-17 in London, but there isn’t much reason for concern in the overall numbers.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="29">The Jaguars remain the most balanced team in the top two, with both their adjusted offense and defense ranking fourth.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="30">Their 68.1 score is barely different from last week, and the gap to San Francisco is just 0.54 points using the unrounded ratings.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="31">The difference is that San Francisco’s offense has separated itself, while Jacksonville has earned its position through quality on both sides of the ball.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="32">DEPI ranks the Jaguars third at +6.15, suggesting their strong early-season performance is supported by a credible forward-looking profile.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/sea.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="33"><strong>3. Seattle Seahawks — 63.4</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary"><span data-source-paragraph="34"><strong>3-2 | Week 4: 3 | Offense: 26 | Defense: 2 | Special teams: 2</strong></span> <span class="efficiency-team-depi">| DEPI: +2.69 (No. 10)</span></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="35">Seattle remains third despite losing to San Francisco, which is a useful reminder that the Efficiency Index is not simply a weekly reward for winning.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="36">The Seahawks have the second-ranked adjusted defense and second-ranked special teams. Those two units are doing an enormous amount of work to compensate for an offense that ranks 26th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="37">That imbalance is difficult to ignore.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="38">Seattle has shown it can make life difficult for good opponents, but the offense continues to limit how consistently it can convert those performances into victories.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="39">The Power Index is considerably less enthusiastic, ranking the Seahawks 10th at +2.69.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="40">One model sees a team performing at a top-three level. The other questions whether that performance is sustainable without more offensive production.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/kc.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="41"><strong>4. Kansas City Chiefs — 62.6</strong></span></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="43">Kansas City remains undefeated following its bye, with a balanced profile that keeps the Chiefs comfortably inside the top five.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="44">Their offense ranks fifth, defense 13th and special teams 10th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="45">Interestingly, their Efficiency score dropped from 64.1 to 62.6 despite not playing.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="46">That is not a penalty for taking a week off. The model evaluates opponent strength and team performance together, so new results elsewhere can change the context surrounding games already played.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="47">The Power Index is notably less impressed, placing Kansas City ninth at +2.71. The Chiefs are unquestionably one of the league’s strongest performers so far, but our forward-looking model sees a team closer to the second tier than the very top.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="50">The Ravens climbed into the top five after their 30-23 overtime victory in Atlanta, a particularly encouraging result considering Lamar Jackson wasn’t available.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/min.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="56"><strong>6. Minnesota Vikings — 61.6</strong></span></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="58">Minnesota might be the most interesting team in football.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="59">The Vikings are undefeated. They have the league’s best adjusted defense and best adjusted special teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="60">They also have the worst adjusted offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="61">That is a remarkable combination for a 5-0 team.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="62">Their 23-13 win over New Orleans continued the formula that has worked throughout September and into October. Prevent scoring, create favorable field position, make enough plays on offense and avoid asking that offense to carry the team.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="63">There is nothing illegitimate about winning that way. Defense and special teams count, and Minnesota has been exceptional in both.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="64">The question is how long it can continue.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="65">DEPI ranks the Vikings just 18th, with an overall rating of -0.31. Their projected offensive contribution is a dreadful -5.28 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="66">Eventually, a team with championship ambitions has to generate offense against quality opponents. Minnesota has been excellent at covering up that weakness, but it hasn’t made the weakness disappear.</p>

<h2 data-source-paragraph="105"><strong>Biggest Fallers: Chicago, Las Vegas and the Rams</strong></h2>

<p data-source-paragraph="106">Four teams fell seven places this week: Chicago, the Rams, Giants and Raiders.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="107">Chicago’s 31-7 defeat at Green Bay produced a 7.56-point Efficiency decline, dropping the Bears from fifth to 12th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="108">That is a significant correction, although their quarterback situation remains important context when trying to project what happens next.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="109">The Raiders suffered the biggest Index-point decline of the week, falling 8.60 points after their 45-17 loss to New England. Las Vegas dropped from 20th to 27th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="111">The Rams present a more complicated case.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="112">They fell from 10th to 17th in Efficiency despite a competitive 37-34 loss to Buffalo. Their current-season performance no longer resembles that of a top-tier team.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="114">It is possible the Rams remain considerably better than their record and current performance suggest.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="115">It is also possible that the Power Index is taking too long to acknowledge a meaningful decline.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="116">That is exactly the kind of disagreement worth tracking as the season continues.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="134"><strong>Pittsburgh at Tampa Bay: A disagreement worth investigating</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="135">This is the biggest disagreement on the board, and it isn’t particularly close.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="136">The market favors Pittsburgh by 2.5 points on the road. DEPI favors Tampa Bay by approximately four points at home.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="137">That’s a 6.5-point difference, with the two sides not even agreeing about which team should be favored.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="138">Some of that difference is easy to understand. Tampa Bay has improved substantially in our ratings, especially defensively, while Pittsburgh sits 19th in the Power Index at -1.11.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="139">But there is a crucial complication.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="140">DEPI’s default quarterback assumption for Tampa Bay is Baker Mayfield, who has been recovering from a dislocated thumb. Rookie Jalon Daniels started the previous two games, and Mayfield’s Week 6 availability has not yet been confirmed.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="141">The model still favors Tampa if Daniels starts, but by a much narrower 1.6 points.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="142"><strong>Washington at San Francisco: Is the market underestimating the 49ers?</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="143">DEPI makes San Francisco a 13.3-point favorite over Washington. The market line is 49ers -7.5.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="144">That’s a difference of nearly six points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="145">The 49ers have the best adjusted offense in the Efficiency Index and the strongest overall Power rating by a comfortable margin. Washington, meanwhile, is 22nd in DEPI at -1.83.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="146">The calculation is straightforward, but the question is whether San Francisco’s offensive dominance justifies laying nearly two touchdowns rather than the market’s more modest number.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="147">Large favorites introduce their own complications. Teams with substantial leads can become conservative, reduce possessions and allow late scores that narrow the final margin without meaningfully threatening the result.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="148">Still, this is one of the clearest cases where DEPI sees a much larger talent and performance gap than the betting market.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="149"><strong>Houston at Jacksonville: The model prefers the Jaguars</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="150">Jacksonville is favored by 2.5 points in London, but DEPI projects the Jaguars to win by approximately 7.6.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="151">That creates a 5.1-point difference.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="152">Importantly, this is a neutral-site calculation. The game is being played at Wembley Stadium, so the model does not award Jacksonville the normal 1.57-point home-field advantage.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="153">The Jaguars’ fourth-ranked adjusted offense and defense support a stronger rating than Houston’s, despite the Texans showing signs of improvement with their 26-3 victory over Tennessee.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="154">There are reasons to be cautious.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="155">Jacksonville will be without starting left tackle Anton Harrison following his knee injury against Philadelphia. Walker Little is expected to replace him. The Jaguars also have the familiarity of a second consecutive London game, something the neutral-site adjustment does not explicitly reward.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="156">The gap is substantial, but the model cannot account for every important change in the matchup.</p>

<h3 data-source-paragraph="157"><strong>Buffalo at Las Vegas: The Bills deserve to be bigger favorites</strong></h3>

<p data-source-paragraph="158">Buffalo’s rise to second in the Power Index has created a substantial difference with the Week 6 line.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="159">The Bills are favored by 5.5 points in Las Vegas. DEPI makes them approximately 10-point favorites.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="160">Buffalo’s projected offensive contribution is +8.05 points, among the strongest in the league, while Las Vegas has fallen to 26th in DEPI after its disastrous defensive performance against New England.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="161">That is a difficult combination for the Raiders.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="162">Buffalo has defensive problems of its own, of course, and those could keep Las Vegas competitive. But the Power Index sees the offensive mismatch as considerably more important.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="163">Of the larger discrepancies, this is one of the cleaner cases where the model’s underlying explanation is easy to follow.</p>
