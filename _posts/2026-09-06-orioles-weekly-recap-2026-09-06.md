Mercifully, the Orioles let their season die just in time for Baltimore to turn its attention to the Ravens' Week 1 matchup with Indianapolis.

For a brief moment, the Orioles seemed right back in it. Monday night's win in Colorado brought the Birds back to .500 for the first time since April and within a half-game of the final American League Wild Card spot. After everything that had happened this season, somehow they had dragged themselves back into legitimate contention.

They then lost the next six games.

The O's dropped the final two games against one of the worst teams in baseball in Colorado, returned home and got swept four straight by Boston, ending the week at 69-75. They are now four games behind Cleveland for the final Wild Card with 18 games remaining.

It was not primarily a pitching failure. The Orioles scored 15 runs in seven games, and five of them came in Thursday's series opener against Boston. Over the final three games of the week, they scored one run. Total.

The pitching staff repeatedly gave them chances to win. The offense simply did almost nothing with them.

## A week that fell apart

Monday provided the lone happy memory. Cade Povich allowed one run over five innings at Coors Field, Samuel Basallo doubled home Dylan Beavers to tie the game in the fifth and Colton Cowser drove in the go-ahead run in the seventh. The real highlight came with two outs in the ninth, when Cowser climbed the center-field wall and robbed Jake McCarthy of a walk-off two-run homer, helping Andrew Kittredge close out a 2-1 win and putting the O's at 69-69.

Tuesday, Kyle Bradish battled through five innings and allowed two runs despite shaky command, while Jackson Holliday went deep as part of a three-hit game. The Orioles nevertheless managed only two runs at Coors Field against a Rockies club that entered the night riding a franchise-record 10-game home losing streak. A Holliday error extended the seventh, and rookie Luis De Leon surrendered a two-run homer to TJ Rumfield in a 4-2 loss.

Wednesday was more aggravating because Trevor Rogers was outstanding. He struck out a career-high 11 over 6 2/3 innings while walking nobody and allowing two runs. The O's built an early lead behind Pete Alonso and newly acquired Luis Robert Jr., then watched the bullpen give it away. Even after Baltimore scored twice in the top of the 11th, Colorado answered with three in the bottom half to win 6-5. A series that began with the Orioles reaching .500 ended with them losing two of three to the Rockies.

Things got worse when Adley Rutschman returned to Camden Yards Thursday wearing a Red Sox uniform. Brandon Young gave Baltimore another respectable start, allowing three runs over 6 2/3 innings with seven strikeouts. Luis Robert Jr. hit his first homer as an Oriole, and Alonso immediately followed with another to give the O's a 3-1 lead. But Rutschman homered twice in his return, Boston tied the game, and Nick Sogard hit a two-run homer off Yennier Cano in the eighth. Roman Anthony added another in the ninth before Encarnacion-Strand's two-run shot made it a 6-5 final.

Friday, the offense disappeared as Shane Baz allowed a home run to the second batter of the game, then spent the rest of the night essentially shutting Boston down. He finished with 6 1/3 innings of one-run ball, four hits allowed and six strikeouts. He got no run support, resulting in a 1-0 loss. The O's collected six singles, three from Blaze Alexander, and never scored.

Saturday continued the pain. Chris Bassitt matched Sonny Gray through five scoreless innings before allowing three runs in the sixth. The Red Sox added two more against the bullpen, but it hardly mattered because the Orioles were shut out again. They managed five hits and went 0-for-8 with runners in scoring position in a 5-0 loss.

Sunday offered one final chance to salvage something. Encarnacion-Strand doubled home Coby Mayo in the first to give Baltimore an immediate 1-0 lead. Bradish gave up no earned runs, but a Gunnar Henderson error extended the second inning before Eli White hit a three-run homer. Bradish lasted only four innings because he needed 85 pitches, while Anthony Nunez, Luis De Leon, Cam Sanders and Grant Wolfram combined to blank Boston the rest of the way.

Again, it did not matter. Red Sox rookie Payton Tolle struck out 12 over six innings, Aroldis Chapman recorded his 400th career save and Boston completed the four-game sweep with a 3-1 win.

## Player of the Week: Christian Encarnacion-Strand

This was not exactly an award with fierce competition.

Encarnacion-Strand went 6-for-21 with two doubles, a home run and three RBIs, producing a .286/.318/.524 slash line and an .842 OPS. His biggest swing came Thursday, when his two-run homer off Chapman in the ninth gave the Orioles one last chance against Boston. He also drove in Baltimore's only run Sunday with a first-inning double.

An .842 OPS is normally a nice week. This week it made Encarnacion-Strand look like Barry Bonds compared with most of his teammates.

Coby Mayo deserves the strongest offensive honorable mention after going 5-for-16 with a double and two walks, good for a .312 average and .389 on-base percentage. There was not much power, but simply reaching base consistently qualified as a major accomplishment this week.

The better honorable mentions are on the mound. Rogers, Baz and Bradish all gave the Orioles opportunities to win. Walker and Sanders combined for 5 2/3 scoreless relief innings with 10 strikeouts. The pitching was not perfect, but it also was not why the Orioles went 1-6.

## Down on the farm

The best news in the organization continues to come from Chesapeake. Vance Honeycutt had a monster week, going 9-for-22 with three doubles, two home runs, five RBIs and three stolen bases. He finished with a .409/.458/.818 slash line and a 1.276 OPS. His speed and defense have always provided a path to the majors. If this kind of offensive production becomes even remotely consistent, his ceiling changes considerably.

Wehiwa Aloy supplied even more power, hitting three home runs and driving in five while stealing two bases. He hit .238 for the week, but three homers in 22 plate appearances will play. Anthony Eyanson delivered perhaps the most encouraging pitching performance, allowing one run over six innings while striking out eight without issuing a walk. Patrick Reilly was excellent as well, working seven innings with four hits, one walk and eight strikeouts while allowing two runs. Caden Hunter struck out nine over five innings and JT Quinn threw five scoreless.

At Norfolk, Enrique Bradfield Jr. continued doing what he needs to do to build a major-league case. He hit .292 with a .393 OBP, two doubles, a triple and four stolen bases. The power is never going to be the selling point. Getting on base, creating havoc with his legs and playing high-level defense is the formula.

The pitching news at Norfolk was less encouraging. Povich was tagged for eight runs in five innings, while Michael Forret allowed two runs in three innings with three walks. Felix Bautista continued his rehab work, allowing one run over 1 2/3 innings with three walks and three strikeouts. At this point the results matter less than the health, but the command clearly still has some distance to go.
