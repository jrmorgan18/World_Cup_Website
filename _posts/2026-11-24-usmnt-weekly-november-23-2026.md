<p data-draft-paragraph="2">The first weekend back from the international break produced some outstanding performances from American players, with several established USMNT starters picking up where they left off and a few younger players continuing to push their way into the conversation.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="3">The biggest performance came in the MLS playoffs, where 19-year-old Peyton Miller was the star of New England&#x27;s 2-0 victory over Philadelphia. Playing in a more advanced role on the wing, Miller created the opening goal and scored an excellent second to give the Revolution an early advantage in their first-round series.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="4">Christian Pulisic continued his outstanding form for AC Milan, scoring a beautiful goal and helping create two others in a 5-1 demolition of Frosinone. Malik Tillman was one of Bayer Leverkusen&#x27;s most influential attackers in a comfortable Bundesliga victory, while Johnny Cardoso scored and delivered another encouraging midfield performance for Atlético Madrid.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="5">Perhaps the most interesting performance came from Alex Freeman. Villarreal blew an early 2-0 lead and lost 3-2 at Barcelona, but the young American was excellent for much of the game, using his athleticism and defensive instincts to frustrate Barcelona&#x27;s attack, particularly Karim Adeyemi.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="6">There were encouraging developments elsewhere. Brenden Aaronson scored off the bench in Leeds&#x27; impressive victory at Chelsea, Sebastian Berhalter added another assist for Middlesbrough, and Mathis Albert helped create Borussia Dortmund&#x27;s late winner.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="7">The negatives were mostly concentrated around injuries and playing time. Mark McKenzie left Toulouse&#x27;s game at halftime with a reported hamstring problem, while Gio Reyna, Haji Wright and Kevin Paredes remain sidelined. Folarin Balogun is still struggling to secure a regular starting role at Monaco, and Tim Weah was part of another disappointing Marseille collapse.</p>

<h2 id="spotlight-players" data-draft-paragraph="18"><strong>Spotlight Players</strong></h2>

<h3 id="peyton-miller-new-england-revolution" data-draft-paragraph="19"><span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/peyton-miller.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy"><strong>Peyton Miller, New England Revolution</strong></span></h3>

<p data-draft-paragraph="20">Miller delivered one of the best performances of his young career Saturday, leading New England to a 2-0 victory over Philadelphia in the opening game of their MLS playoff series.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="21">The 19-year-old started in an advanced role on the right wing rather than his more familiar left-sided defensive position, and he immediately looked comfortable attacking the Union defense.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="22">Just ten minutes into the match, Miller helped create the opening goal. Carles Gil played a ball through the Philadelphia defense and Miller made an excellent run into the available space, getting himself into a dangerous shooting position.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="23">The actual finish left something to be desired.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="24">Miller badly scuffed his shot, but it took a fortunate deflection off Philadelphia defender Nathan Harriel and trickled into the net for an own goal. There was certainly some luck involved, but the movement that created the opportunity was excellent. Miller recognized the opening, accelerated into the box and forced the defender into a difficult situation.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="25">He nearly added another goal in the 30th minute.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="26">New England broke forward down the left side and Dor Turgeman delivered a dangerous ball across the penalty area. Miller again made an excellent run to get into scoring position and this time struck the ball cleanly, forcing a terrific kick save from Philadelphia goalkeeper Andrew Rick.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="27">Beyond the attacking moments, Miller was active defensively. He helped New England&#x27;s press by closing down the Union&#x27;s buildup and putting pressure on defenders before they could comfortably advance the ball.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="28">In the 62nd minute, New England introduced Jack Harrison, returning from a lengthy injury absence. Miller moved over to the left wing, but the positional change did nothing to reduce his influence.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="29">Ten minutes later came the best moment of the match.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="30">Harrison received the ball on the right and delivered a beautifully placed cross toward the back post. Miller recognized the space behind the Philadelphia defense, arrived unmarked and hit perfectly struck left-footed volley off the underside of the crossbar and into the net.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="31">The goal effectively sealed the victory and made Miller the fifth-youngest player to score in MLS playoff history. He finished with two shots on target and two key passes, in addition to creating the opening own goal.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="32">New England will travel to Philadelphia next Saturday with an opportunity to close out the series. Miller has given the Revolution a pretty good reason to keep feeding him the ball.</p>

<h3 id="christian-pulisic-ac-milan" data-draft-paragraph="33"><span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/christian-pulisic.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy"><strong>Christian Pulisic, AC Milan</strong></span></h3>

<p data-draft-paragraph="34">Pulisic&#x27;s outstanding run of form continued Sunday as AC Milan demolished Frosinone 5-1, with the American contributing to several dangerous attacks before scoring an excellent goal of his own.</p>

<figure class="soccer-article-figure soccer-article-figure--uncropped"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/usmnt-weekly-2026-11-24/christian-pulisic-frosinone.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Christian Pulisic carries the ball against Frosinone at San Siro" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Christian Pulisic in action during Milan’s win over Frosinone. <p data-draft-paragraph="35">He started on the left side of Milan&#x27;s attack and was involved almost immediately.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="36">In the fourth minute, Milan broke forward through Adrien Rabiot. Pulisic recognized the developing opportunity and made a sharp run toward the penalty area, forcing Frosinone defender Gabriele Calvani to account for his movement.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="37">Rabiot played the ball toward the American, but Calvani got there first and inadvertently directed it into his own net. Pulisic doesn&#x27;t receive a goal contribution for the play, but his run helped create the problem for the defender. Without the threat of Pulisic arriving behind him, Calvani would have been in a much less difficult position.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="38">Milan eventually took a 2-1 lead into halftime before breaking the game open after the restart. Pulisic was involved again around the hour mark, playing an important pass to help launch the counterattack that led to Milan&#x27;s fourth goal. Diego Moreira eventually delivered the final ball to Alphadjo Cissé for the finish.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="39">Then, in the 68th minute, Pulisic produced the best individual moment of his afternoon. Frosinone had pushed players forward for a corner when the resulting possession broke down. Pulisic recognized the opportunity, reacted quickly to a poorly controlled ball from Aleksa Terzić and accelerated into the open field.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="40">Pulisic won the footrace, carried the ball toward goal and then calmly lifted a delicate chip over goalkeeper Lorenzo Palmisani to make it 5-1. It was an outstanding combination of anticipation, speed and composure.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="41">The Italian reviews were very positive, although not unanimously glowing. SempreMilan awarded him an 8/10, praising his attacking movement and ability to find dangerous spaces. Sport Mediaset gave him a 7, noting that he was somewhat quieter during portions of the game before delivering the decisive moments.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="42">He has now scored in three consecutive Serie A appearances, giving him five league goals this season. More importantly, Pulisic has settled into an effective role in Rúben Amorim&#x27;s attack. He isn&#x27;t simply standing wide waiting to receive possession. He moves between the lines, makes diagonal runs into the box and consistently recognizes when Milan has an opportunity to attack before the opposing defense gets organized.</p>

<h3 id="alex-freeman-villarreal" data-draft-paragraph="43"><span class="article-spotlight-name"><img class="article-spotlight-avatar" src="{{ '/assets/images/player-pool/alex-freeman.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy"><strong>Alex Freeman, Villarreal</strong></span></h3>

<p data-draft-paragraph="44">Villarreal suffered one of the more painful defeats of the weekend, surrendering a 2-0 lead at Barcelona and eventually losing 3-2.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="45">However, Alex Freeman&#x27;s individual performance was solid. Villarreal stunned Barcelona by scoring twice in the opening seven minutes, building a surprising early advantage at Camp Nou.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="46">Freeman played an important role in the second goal. In the sixth minute, he won possession twice during the sequence that launched Villarreal&#x27;s counterattack. His aggressive defensive interventions helped create the transition opportunity that eventually ended with Tani Oluwaseyi making it 2-0. It was a good example of how Freeman can influence a game without necessarily contributing the final pass or shot.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="47">Throughout much of the match, Freeman was excellent defensively. Playing at right back, he frequently found himself matched up against Karim Adeyemi, one of Barcelona&#x27;s most explosive attacking players. Adeyemi is exceptionally dangerous when he can receive possession and accelerate past a defender, but Freeman largely prevented him from creating those situations.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="48">Freeman used his size and strength to absorb contact, while having enough speed to stay with Adeyemi when the Barcelona winger attempted to attack space behind him.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="49">He also made several important interventions to prevent dangerous opportunities from developing, often stepping in to disrupt possession before Barcelona could turn an attack into a clean chance.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="50">Barcelona eventually came back with three goals in a ten-minute stretch during the second half, but Freeman wasn&#x27;t directly responsible for any of them. The equalizing goals came through Gerard Martín and Fermín López, before an unfortunate own goal by Pau Navarro completed the comeback.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="51">Freeman finished with three successful tackles from five attempts and was one of Villarreal&#x27;s stronger individual defenders.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="52">He has now moved well beyond the stage where simply getting on the field for Villarreal is an accomplishment. The next challenge is turning these strong defensive performances into a more complete two-way game.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="54">Tillman produced one of his better performances of the season Saturday as Bayer Leverkusen comfortably defeated Paderborn 4-0. He started in an advanced midfield role and was dangerous almost immediately.</p>

<figure class="soccer-article-figure soccer-article-figure--uncropped"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/usmnt-weekly-2026-11-24/malik-tillman-leverkusen.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Malik Tillman in Bayer Leverkusen training wear" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Malik Tillman in Leverkusen colours. <p data-draft-paragraph="55">Early in the first half, he got onto the end of an Aleix García cross inside the penalty area and directed a header toward goal, forcing a good reaction save from goalkeeper Nahuel Noll. It was a well-timed run and a legitimate scoring opportunity, even if the finish wasn&#x27;t quite enough to beat the keeper.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="56">Tillman made his next major contribution in the 14th minute. Receiving possession in an advanced position, he recognized Afonso Moreira making a run into the penalty area and delivered a nicely weighted chipped ball over the defense.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="57">Moreira finished the opportunity to give Leverkusen an early 1-0 lead.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="58">The pass was the kind of creative action that makes Tillman an interesting player for both club and country. Rather than simply circulating possession around the defense, he recognized the movement ahead of him and had the technique to put the ball into exactly the right space.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="199">The positive is that Tessmann played the full match in the new coach&#x27;s first league game.</p>

<p data-draft-paragraph="200">Whether he can turn that opportunity into a regular starting position remains to be seen.</p>
