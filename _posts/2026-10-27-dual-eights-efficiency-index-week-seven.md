<p data-source-paragraph="2"><strong>October 27, 2026 | Through Week 7</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="3">The San Francisco 49ers finally lost a football game Sunday. According to the Dual Eights Efficiency Index, they&#x27;re still the best team in the NFL.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="4">San Francisco&#x27;s 29-20 loss in Atlanta trimmed its lead, but seven weeks of consistently strong offensive performance have given the 49ers enough of a cushion to remain first in both our Efficiency and Power rankings.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="5">Elsewhere, Week 7 produced some significant movement.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="6">Seattle&#x27;s defense delivered the most impressive performance of the week, holding the previously unbeaten Kansas City Chiefs to three points and pushing the Seahawks into third. Tampa Bay demolished Carolina 43-13 and jumped six places into the top 10, despite having a 2-5 record.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="7">The Buccaneers are now sixth in Efficiency. Yes, sixth.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="8">Meanwhile, Minnesota remains undefeated at 6-0 but continues to rank considerably lower in our forward-looking Power Index. The Vikings have one of the league&#x27;s best defenses and one of its worst offenses, a combination that keeps winning games without entirely convincing the model that Minnesota belongs among the NFL&#x27;s elite.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="9">And then there are the Ravens.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="10">Baltimore&#x27;s 35-13 collapse against Cincinnati dropped the Ravens three positions to 14th, their lowest Efficiency ranking since we introduced the Power Index. However, DEPI still places them eighth, provided Lamar Jackson is healthy enough to return.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="11">That distinction between what teams have actually produced and what we expect them to do next is becoming increasingly important.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/sf.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="15"><strong>1. San Francisco 49ers — 68.6</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="16"><strong>6-1 | Week 6: 1 | DEPI: 1 (+7.37)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="17">The 49ers finally stumbled, but their first defeat wasn&#x27;t enough to knock them from the top.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="18">Atlanta&#x27;s 29-20 victory reduced San Francisco&#x27;s Efficiency rating by 0.84 points, narrowing its lead over Jacksonville to 1.46. That&#x27;s a meaningful difference, but not enough to overcome everything the 49ers accomplished through their opening six victories.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="19">San Francisco&#x27;s offense remains the foundation. The 49ers are averaging +0.175 expected points added (EPA) per offensive play, with a success rate of 53.3%. That means they are consistently creating positive situations rather than depending entirely on occasional explosive plays.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="20">Interestingly, the model&#x27;s opponent-adjusted offensive component actually improved slightly this week. The decline came primarily from defense and special teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="21">There are legitimate reasons to reassess a team after its first loss, particularly when the defense struggles. But there is little reason to discard six weeks of excellent performance because Atlanta produced an upset.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="22">DEPI agrees, keeping the 49ers first at +7.37 points. That rating did decline by approximately 0.78 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="23">San Francisco remains the team everyone else is chasing.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/jax.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="24"><strong>2. Jacksonville Jaguars — 67.1</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="25"><strong>4-2 | Week 6: 2 | DEPI: 6 (+4.06)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="26">Jacksonville didn&#x27;t play in Week 7, but the Jaguars remain close enough to San Francisco that another strong performance could restore their position at the top.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="27">The Jaguars&#x27; offensive efficiency stands at +0.158 EPA per play, with the defense allowing -0.033. That&#x27;s a particularly appealing combination because it doesn&#x27;t require one unit to compensate for severe weaknesses elsewhere.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="28">Jacksonville&#x27;s second-place Efficiency ranking makes sense based on its current production.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="29">The Power Index is a little more cautious.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="30">DEPI ranks the Jaguars sixth at +4.06, indicating that their current-season performance is stronger than the model&#x27;s expectation for future games.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="31">That isn&#x27;t necessarily a criticism. Jacksonville has demonstrated that it can play at an elite level. The question is whether seven weeks provide enough evidence to assume that level will continue.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="32">The Jaguars fell from fourth to sixth in DEPI during their bye, a change driven by the model&#x27;s reassessment of team strength across the league rather than anything Jacksonville did on the field.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/sea.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="33"><strong>3. Seattle Seahawks — 65.4</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="34"><strong>4-3 | Week 6: 5 | DEPI: 9 (+2.76)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="35">Seattle made the loudest statement of Week 7.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="36">Holding the undefeated Chiefs to three points in a 20-3 victory is impressive under almost any circumstances. Doing it against an offense that had averaged +0.121 EPA per play entering the updated rankings is even more convincing.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="37">Seattle&#x27;s defense has now allowed -0.134 EPA per play this season, while opposing offenses have succeeded on just 36.5% of their snaps. The Week 7 performance improved Seattle&#x27;s opponent-adjusted offense and defense, lifting its overall Efficiency score by 3.45 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="38">What keeps the Seahawks from being ranked higher in DEPI is their offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="39">The forward-looking model values Seattle&#x27;s defense at +3.08 points and special teams at +0.79, but projects the offense to contribute 1.10 points below average.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="40">That leaves Seattle ninth in DEPI despite ranking third in Efficiency.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="41">There is a reasonable argument that an exceptional defense and strong special teams can carry a team further than the model currently expects. Seattle just demonstrated that against one of the NFL&#x27;s best opponents.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="42">But there is also a difference between winning games with dominant defense and consistently beating other elite teams when the defense isn&#x27;t having its best afternoon.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="43">The Seahawks have established their defensive credentials. Their offensive limitations remain the question.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/chi.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="44"><strong>4. Chicago Bears — 63.3</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="45"><strong>5-2 | Week 6: 4 | DEPI: 4 (+4.60)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="46">Chicago continues to look like one of the NFC&#x27;s more credible contenders, improving to 5-2 with a 30-24 victory over New England.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="47">The Bears remain fourth in Efficiency, although their score actually declined slightly following the victory.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="48">The primary reason was a weaker opponent-adjusted defensive performance. Chicago&#x27;s defense is allowing opposing offenses to succeed on 41.2% of plays, while its own offense has generated +0.049 EPA per play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="49">Those numbers are solid rather than overwhelming, but the Bears have assembled a balanced enough profile to consistently compete.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="50">Unlike Seattle or Minnesota, there isn&#x27;t a substantial disagreement between the two indexes.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="51">DEPI also ranks Chicago fourth at +4.60. The Rams are third at +4.63, a difference of less than four hundredths of a point.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="52">For practical purposes, those teams are virtually tied.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="53">Chicago&#x27;s Week 8 trip to Seattle should be particularly interesting. The Efficiency rankings favor the Seahawks, while the Power Index gives Chicago a slight advantage even after accounting for Seattle&#x27;s home field.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="54">We&#x27;ll return to that matchup later.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/cin.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="55"><strong>5. Cincinnati Bengals — 62.7</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="56"><strong>4-2 | Week 6: 7 | DEPI: 5 (+4.16)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="57">The Bengals provided Ravens fans with an extremely unpleasant demonstration of their offensive capabilities Sunday. Cincinnati entered halftime trailing 13-0, then scored 35 unanswered points to turn what appeared to be a promising afternoon for Baltimore into a blowout.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="58">The Bengals&#x27; offense generated approximately +0.331 EPA per play against the Ravens, an exceptional single-game number. Their opponent-adjusted defense also improved, though that performance needs to be viewed in the context of Tyler Huntley starting instead of Lamar Jackson.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="59">The result added 3.62 points to Cincinnati&#x27;s Efficiency rating and lifted the Bengals two positions into the top five. For the season, Cincinnati&#x27;s offense is averaging +0.109 EPA per play with a 48.5% success rate.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="60">The more encouraging development for Cincinnati is that both sides of the ball contributed to the improvement. The offense has enough talent to overwhelm opponents when the protection and timing are functioning. If the defense can consistently put opposing offenses into difficult situations, the Bengals become considerably more dangerous.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="61">DEPI ranks Cincinnati fifth at +4.16, making it one of the teams where current production and future expectations align almost perfectly.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="62">That&#x27;s not great news for the Ravens.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/tb.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="63"><strong>6. Tampa Bay Buccaneers — 61.3</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="64"><strong>2-5 | Week 6: 12 | DEPI: 10 (+2.10)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="65">Tampa Bay might be the most confusing team in football.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="66">The Buccaneers are 2-5. They are also sixth in our Efficiency Index following the largest improvement of Week 7.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="67">Their 43-13 demolition of Carolina improved the index by 6.16 points and moved Tampa Bay up six positions.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="68">What makes the improvement particularly encouraging is that all three components contributed. The defense supplied the biggest gain, the offense wasn&#x27;t far behind, and special teams also improved.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="69">Tampa Bay&#x27;s defense is now allowing -0.109 EPA per play over the season, which is the strongest argument for taking the Buccaneers seriously despite their record. The offense, however, is still averaging -0.081 EPA per play. One excellent afternoon doesn&#x27;t erase an otherwise inefficient start.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="70">The Buccaneers have demonstrated enough defensive ability, and enough improvement elsewhere, to suggest they are better than their record. Though DEPI is somewhat less enthusiastic, ranking them 10th at +2.10.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="71">But both models now view Tampa Bay as an above-average team.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="72">At some point, the Buccaneers need to translate that underlying improvement into actual victories. A 2-5 record still creates a difficult path to the postseason, regardless of how impressive the efficiency numbers become.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/kc.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="73"><strong>7. Kansas City Chiefs — 60.1</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="74"><strong>5-1 | Week 6: 3 | DEPI: 12 (+1.27)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="75">Kansas City&#x27;s unbeaten start came to an abrupt end in Seattle, and the Efficiency Index responded.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="76">The Chiefs lost 5.21 index points, dropping four places to seventh.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="77">The model recorded deterioration in all three components, with the opponent-adjusted defense contributing the largest share of the decline.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="78">Of course, the offense scoring three points wasn&#x27;t exactly a ringing endorsement of its afternoon, either.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="79">Kansas City still averages +0.121 offensive EPA per play for the season, which is a reminder that one awful outing does not erase everything the unit has done.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="80">But the Power Index has been skeptical of the Chiefs for several weeks.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="81">Despite a 5-1 record, Kansas City ranks only 12th in DEPI at +1.27. That rating fell from approximately +2.75 entering the week.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="82">This is a substantial disagreement between reputation, record and underlying projections.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="83">The Chiefs have demonstrated they can win games. What they haven&#x27;t consistently demonstrated, according to our models, is the level of dominance we normally associate with a top-tier championship contender.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="84">Seattle exposed that gap rather dramatically.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/min.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="85"><strong>8. Minnesota Vikings — 59.5</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="86"><strong>6-0 | Week 6: 6 | DEPI: 16 (-0.22)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="87">Minnesota remains undefeated.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="88">The Vikings are also one of the league&#x27;s least efficient offensive teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="89">Both statements are true, and figuring out how long they can coexist is becoming one of the more interesting questions of the season.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="90">Minnesota beat Indianapolis 16-10 to improve to 6-0, but its Efficiency score declined by 2.31 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="91">The defense remains extraordinary.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="92">The Vikings allow -0.197 EPA per play, with opposing offenses succeeding on just 34.9% of snaps. That&#x27;s the kind of defensive performance that can win games almost independently.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="93">Unfortunately, the offense appears increasingly willing to test that theory.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="94">Minnesota is averaging -0.090 EPA per offensive play with a success rate of just 36.0%.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="95">DEPI quantifies the imbalance. It projects the offense at -4.05 points, partially offset by a +2.81 defensive contribution and +1.00 from special teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="96">The result is a Power Index rating of -0.22, placing the undefeated Vikings 16th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="97">There is a legitimate counterargument here.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="98">Minnesota has won six games. Defense and special teams are real football, not statistical inconveniences. And continually forcing opponents into inefficient possessions can be a sustainable formula when the defensive talent and execution are good enough.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="99">But championship-caliber offenses eventually find ways to score, even against excellent defenses. When that happens, can Minnesota consistently answer?</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="100">The Vikings have earned every one of their victories. They haven&#x27;t yet demonstrated that their current formula provides a realistic championship ceiling.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/det.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="101"><strong>9. Detroit Lions — 58.3</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="102"><strong>4-2 | Week 6: 13 | DEPI: 7 (+3.05)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="103">Detroit returns to the top 10 following a hard-fought 20-17 victory over Green Bay.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="104">The Lions climbed four positions and gained 3.53 Efficiency points, with the defense and special teams providing most of the improvement.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="105">That&#x27;s encouraging because Detroit&#x27;s offense has never really been the biggest concern.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="106">The Lions are averaging +0.164 EPA per offensive play with a 50.0% success rate. Those are excellent numbers, comparable to the production of teams ranked considerably higher.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="107">The defense remains the problem.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="108">Detroit is allowing +0.140 EPA per play, a poor number for a team with serious championship ambitions.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="109">One encouraging defensive performance doesn&#x27;t suddenly resolve a season-long weakness. But the Lions don&#x27;t necessarily need their defense to become dominant. They need it to become competent enough that their offense isn&#x27;t constantly forced to overcome what happens on the other side of the ball.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="110">DEPI ranks Detroit seventh at +3.05, slightly ahead of its current Efficiency position.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="111">The Power Index sees an offensive foundation strong enough to keep the Lions among the NFC contenders.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="112">If the defense continues improving, Detroit has considerable room to climb.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/buf.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="113"><strong>10. Buffalo Bills — 56.7</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="114"><strong>5-1 | Week 6: 9 | DEPI: 2 (+4.95)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="115">Buffalo rounds out the top 10 despite spending Week 7 on a bye.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="116">The Bills are another team whose offensive and defensive profiles point in dramatically different directions.</p>

<h2 id="matchup-to-watch" data-source-paragraph="193"><strong>Week 8 Matchup to Watch: Chicago at Seattle</strong></h2>

<p data-source-paragraph="194">The most interesting matchup from a model perspective is Monday night&#x27;s meeting between the Bears and Seahawks.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="195">Both teams rank among the top four in Efficiency.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="196">Seattle just held Kansas City to three points. Chicago has improved to 5-2 and ranks fourth in both indexes.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="197">Yet the market favors Seattle by approximately three to three and a half points at home, while DEPI makes Chicago a very slight favorite.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="198">The explanation comes from the Power ratings.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="199">Chicago is fourth at +4.60, while Seattle sits ninth at +2.76. That gives the Bears a neutral-field advantage of approximately 1.84 points, slightly more than Seattle&#x27;s 1.54-point home-field adjustment.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="200">The projected result is essentially a toss-up, with Chicago favored by about 0.3 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="201">The difference is Seattle&#x27;s offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="202">The Seahawks&#x27; defense is already playing at an elite level, and Sunday&#x27;s performance suggests it can disrupt even the best offenses in the league.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="203">But Seattle&#x27;s offensive projection remains below average. The Power Index questions whether the Seahawks can consistently produce enough scoring to take advantage of what their defense provides.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="204">Chicago represents another useful test.</p>
