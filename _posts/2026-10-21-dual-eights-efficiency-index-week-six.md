<p data-source-paragraph="2"><strong>October 20, 2026 | Through Week 6</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="3">Six weeks into the NFL season, the San Francisco 49ers have separated themselves from the rest of the league.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="4">At 6-0, San Francisco sits atop both the Dual Eights Efficiency Index and our forward-looking Power Index (DEPI). The 49ers have the NFL&#x27;s most efficient adjusted offense, a 2.3-point lead over Jacksonville in Efficiency, and an even more substantial advantage in projected team strength.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="5">But the biggest story this week is Chicago.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="6">The Bears responded to last week&#x27;s embarrassing loss in Green Bay by beating Atlanta 31-23, climbing eight places to fourth in Efficiency and four places to third in DEPI. No team improved more in either index.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="7">Kansas City also continues to climb, with its defense joining Patrick Mahomes and the offense among the league&#x27;s top units. The undefeated Chiefs are now third in Efficiency, although DEPI remains considerably more skeptical.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="8">And then there are the Ravens.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="9">After surviving two games without Lamar Jackson, Baltimore finally paid the price in Cleveland. The 13-10 loss dropped the Ravens from fifth to 11th in Efficiency, their largest decline of the season. DEPI remains more optimistic about their underlying strength, but that optimism comes with a fairly significant condition.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="10">Lamar has to play.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="11">The expanding gap between the two models is becoming one of the more interesting parts of our weekly rankings. Efficiency measures how well teams have actually performed. DEPI estimates how strong they should be going forward, incorporating historical performance, recent form and quarterback availability.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="12">Sometimes the models agree completely. San Francisco is first in both. Other times they produce dramatically different assessments, particularly for teams such as Minnesota, Buffalo and the Rams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="13">Those disagreements give us something useful to examine beyond another weekly list of 32 teams.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/sf.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="17"><strong>1. San Francisco 49ers — 69.4</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="18"><strong>6-0 | Week 5: 1st | Offense: 1 | Defense: 13 | Special teams: 23 | DEPI: 1st (+8.17)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="19">San Francisco continues to build the strongest statistical case for being the best team in football, beating Washington 23-17 on Monday night, to maintain their unbeaten record and increase their Efficiency advantage over Jacksonville to 2.33 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="20">San Francisco leads the league with a 52.7% offensive success rate and ranks second in raw offensive EPA at +0.207 per play. More importantly for this index, its opponent-adjusted offense remains first.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="21">The defense is considerably less imposing at 13th, and special teams rank 23rd. This is not a particularly balanced team, but an offense this consistently productive can cover for plenty of ordinary play elsewhere.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="22">DEPI agrees emphatically, ranking San Francisco first at +8.17, more than three projected points ahead of second-place Buffalo. The offensive component alone accounts for +6.81 of that rating.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="23">There is little disagreement between the models about San Francisco. Both see an exceptional offense that has made the 49ers the strongest team in the league through six weeks.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/jax.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="24"><strong>2. Jacksonville Jaguars — 67.1</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="25"><strong>4-2 | Week 5: 2nd | Offense: 3 | Defense: 6 | Special teams: 14 | DEPI: 4th (+4.42)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="26">Jacksonville lost 34-31 in London to Houston and somehow remained second in Efficiency.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="27">That is not a mistake.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="28">The Jaguars still possess arguably the most balanced profile among the leading contenders, ranking third offensively and sixth defensively. Their special teams have also improved, climbing from 19th to 14th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="29">Giving up 34 points to the Texans certainly hurt. Jacksonville&#x27;s defensive ranking slipped from fourth to sixth, and its overall index declined by 1.06 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="30">But its adjusted offense actually improved from fourth to third. Across six games, the cumulative performance remains strong enough to keep the Jaguars comfortably ahead of Kansas City.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="31">The Power Index is slightly less enthusiastic, placing Jacksonville fourth at +4.42. Its projected offense and defense both make substantial positive contributions, so there is no obvious weakness driving that modest disagreement.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="32">Jacksonville is still a legitimate contender by either measure. The question is whether the defense can return to its earlier form after a disappointing afternoon against Houston.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/kc.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="33"><strong>3. Kansas City Chiefs — 65.3</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="34"><strong>5-0 | Week 5: 4th | Offense: 5 | Defense: 7 | Special teams: 13 | DEPI: 9th (+2.77)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="35">Kansas City keeps winning, and the underlying numbers are beginning to look increasingly impressive.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="36">The Chiefs improved to 5-0 by holding the Chargers to six points in a 22-6 victory. That performance pushed their adjusted defense from 13th to seventh and moved Kansas City into the top three overall.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="37">Their offense remains fifth.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="38">That is now a top-seven team on both sides of the football, rather than an offense dependent upon Mahomes to overcome deficiencies elsewhere.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="39">The interesting part is that DEPI remains unconvinced Kansas City belongs among the league&#x27;s very best.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="40">The Power Index ranks the Chiefs ninth at +2.77, six places below their Efficiency standing. Its projected offensive contribution of +1.80 is solid, but considerably less impressive than their fifth-ranked adjusted offense this season.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="41">Part of that difference comes from DEPI incorporating more historical information and smoothing performance rather than treating the current sample as a definitive measure of future strength.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="42">The counterargument is becoming stronger every week. Kansas City has won all five games, improved defensively and established a much more balanced statistical profile.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="43">At some point, a forecasting model has to recognize sustained improvement rather than remain anchored to its earlier expectations.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="44">The Chiefs have another opportunity to force that adjustment Sunday night in Seattle.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/chi.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="45"><strong>4. Chicago Bears — 63.6</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="46"><strong>4-2 | Week 5: 12th | Offense: 6 | Defense: 15 | Special teams: 5 | DEPI: 3rd (+4.65)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="47">What a difference a week makes.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="48">Last Sunday, Chicago was coming off a 31-7 loss in Green Bay that sent the Bears tumbling from fifth to 12th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="49">This Sunday, with Caleb Williams back under center, they beat Atlanta 31-23 and immediately climbed back into the top five. Their 8.16-point Efficiency improvement was the largest in the NFL, and the eight-place ranking jump was equally impressive.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="50">Unlike some of the more unusual profiles near the top of these rankings, Chicago improved in every phase. The offense climbed from ninth to sixth. The defense jumped from 20th to 15th. Special teams improved from ninth to fifth.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="51">Chicago&#x27;s adjusted offensive ranking deserves some context. The Bears are producing only +0.026 raw offensive EPA per play, which is closer to league average than elite. Their stronger adjusted standing comes from opponent and venue context, along with a respectable 46.7% success rate.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="52">DEPI is also buying the improvement.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="53">Chicago rises from seventh to third in the Power Index at +4.65, with increases of +0.83 projected points on offense and +0.95 on defense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="54">There is still considerable volatility in a six-game sample. The same team has now experienced dramatic movement in opposite directions in consecutive weeks. But unlike last week&#x27;s rankings, where Efficiency and DEPI were diverging after the Green Bay loss, both models now consider Chicago one of the NFL&#x27;s strongest teams.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="55">Thursday&#x27;s matchup with New England should offer another useful test.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/sea.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="56"><strong>5. Seattle Seahawks — 61.9</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="57"><strong>3-3 | Week 5: 3rd | Offense: 26 | Defense: 2 | Special teams: 4 | DEPI: 11th (+1.73)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="58">Seattle remains one of the strangest teams in the top 10.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="59">The Seahawks dropped two places after losing 17-14 at Denver, but their overall efficiency is still fifth despite a .500 record.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="60">The explanation remains largely unchanged.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="61">Seattle has the second-ranked adjusted defense and fourth-ranked special teams, but its offense sits 26th. The offense is producing negative raw EPA per play at -0.079, while the defense consistently gives Seattle an opportunity to remain competitive.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="62">DEPI is less willing to believe that formula makes the Seahawks an elite team going forward. It ranks them 11th at +1.73, with the projected offense actually subtracting 1.83 points from their overall rating.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="63">Efficiency recognizes how much Seattle has accomplished defensively. DEPI questions whether that production can consistently compensate for a bottom-tier offense against quality opponents.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="64">Sunday night&#x27;s visit from Kansas City should help provide an answer.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/min.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="65"><strong>6. Minnesota Vikings — 61.8</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="66"><strong>5-0 | Week 5: 6th | Offense: 31 | Defense: 1 | Special teams: 1 | DEPI: 14th (+0.11)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="67">Minnesota somehow remains undefeated with the NFL&#x27;s second-worst adjusted offense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="68">The Vikings are first in both defense and special teams, which is an extraordinary combination. Their defense leads the NFL in raw EPA allowed at -0.215 per play and opponent success rate allowed at just 33%.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="69">They are exceptionally difficult to move the ball against, and their special teams consistently help control field position.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="70">The offense remains a different story. Even after improving from 32nd to 31st during the bye, Minnesota is nowhere close to producing at the level normally associated with an undefeated contender.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="71">DEPI is particularly skeptical, ranking the Vikings 14th at just +0.11, essentially an average team on a neutral field. Its offensive projection is a dreadful -3.74 points.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="72">That eight-place difference between the two indexes is among the largest in the league.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="73">Minnesota deserves credit for winning games with defense and special teams, but relying on those units to cover for an offense this limited becomes increasingly difficult when opponents are capable of protecting the ball and generating even modest production.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/cin.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="74"><strong>7. Cincinnati Bengals — 59.1</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="75"><strong>3-2 | Week 5: 7th | Offense: 7 | Defense: 12 | Special teams: 12 | DEPI: 7th (+3.36)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="76">Cincinnati had its bye and barely moved.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="77">The Bengals remain seventh in Efficiency with a 59.1 rating, essentially unchanged from last week. Their adjusted offense ranks seventh, while defense and special teams are both 12th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="78">Cincinnati has a good offense supported by two units performing around the upper middle of the league.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="79">DEPI ranks the Bengals seventh as well, at +3.36.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="80">For a ranking exercise increasingly defined by substantial differences between observed performance and projected strength, Cincinnati offers a refreshing lack of controversy.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="81">The Bengals have been good, if not dominant, and both models agree about where they belong.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="82">They visit Baltimore on Sunday in what should be a consequential early AFC North matchup. Depending upon Lamar Jackson&#x27;s availability, the Power Index sees either a narrow Ravens advantage or a slight Bengals edge.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/car.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="83"><strong>8. Carolina Panthers — 58.4</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="84"><strong>3-2 | Week 5: 8th | Offense: 11 | Defense: 11 | Special teams: 11 | DEPI: 13th (+0.64)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="85">Carolina has achieved a rather unusual statistical accomplishment.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="86">The Panthers rank exactly 11th in adjusted offense, defense and special teams. That may be the most balanced profile in the NFL. Their 25-14 victory in Philadelphia improved their overall Efficiency rating by 1.54 points, although they remained eighth.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="87">The defense made the most significant improvement, climbing from 15th to 11th.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="88">There is no individual unit carrying Carolina the way Minnesota&#x27;s defense and special teams carry its struggling offense. The Panthers have simply been consistently above average across the board.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="89">DEPI remains more cautious, ranking Carolina 13th at +0.64.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="90">The five-place difference reflects uncertainty about whether this year&#x27;s balanced performance represents a sustainable improvement over the team&#x27;s historical baseline.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="91">Still, Carolina is moving in the right direction by both measures. Its Power Index rating improved as well, and the Panthers have given themselves a credible foundation without depending upon a single exceptional unit.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="92">That is not the flashiest way to become a playoff contender.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="93">It might be one of the more sustainable ones.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/buf.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="94"><strong>9. Buffalo Bills — 56.9</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="95"><strong>5-1 | Week 5: 10th | Offense: 2 | Defense: 30 | Special teams: 2 | DEPI: 2nd (+5.14)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="96">Buffalo remains one of the largest disagreements between the two models.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="97">The Bills are 5-1 and have the NFL&#x27;s second-ranked adjusted offense and special teams, but they also have the 30th-ranked defense.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="98">Their 33-30 victory in Las Vegas was another demonstration of the problem. Buffalo can produce enough offense to overcome a great deal, but allowing 30 points to the Raiders is hardly encouraging.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="99">The Bills are surrendering +0.135 raw EPA per defensive play, a concerning number for a team with legitimate championship expectations. Efficiency therefore ranks Buffalo only ninth.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="100">DEPI takes a substantially more optimistic position, placing the Bills second at +5.14.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="101">Josh Allen and the offense provide much of the explanation. Buffalo&#x27;s projected offensive contribution is +5.72 points, while the defensive component subtracts 1.60.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="102">Importantly, DEPI does recognize the defensive weakness. It simply considers the offense valuable enough to overcome it more consistently than the overall Efficiency ranking suggests.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="103">The Bills remain one of football&#x27;s most dangerous teams, but their defense is beginning to look like a weakness that could eventually become very expensive.</p>

<h3 class="efficiency-team-heading"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/nfl-logos/ne.png' | relative_url }}" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><span data-source-paragraph="104"><strong>10. New England Patriots — 56.7</strong></span></h3>

<p class="efficiency-team-summary" data-source-paragraph="105"><strong>4-2 | Week 5: 11th | Offense: 12 | Defense: 10 | Special teams: 20 | DEPI: 8th (+2.79)</strong></p>

<p data-source-paragraph="106">New England returns to the top 10 following a 26-19 victory over the Jets.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="107">The Patriots don&#x27;t have a particularly spectacular profile, but they are increasingly well-rounded.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="108">Their adjusted offense ranks 12th and defense 10th. Special teams remain below average at 20th, although that unit improved six places this week.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="109">New England&#x27;s Efficiency score climbed to 56.7, placing it just 0.37 points ahead of the Ravens for the final top-10 position.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="110">DEPI is slightly more optimistic, ranking the Patriots eighth at +2.79.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="111">That agreement is encouraging for a team that continues to improve. New England no longer looks like an early-season surprise living off one outstanding performance.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="112">The Patriots have become a credible top-10 team across both measures.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="113">Thursday night&#x27;s visit to Chicago is particularly interesting because it is the only Week 7 matchup between two teams ranked in the top 10 of both indexes.</p>

<h2 id="risers" data-source-paragraph="114"><strong>Biggest Risers: Chicago Leads, Cleveland Makes Its Move</strong></h2>

<p data-source-paragraph="115">Chicago was comfortably the biggest winner in this week&#x27;s rankings.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="116">The Bears gained 8.16 Efficiency points and eight ranking positions, climbing from 12th to fourth. Their DEPI improvement was similarly impressive, moving from seventh to third.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="121">The standings say the division is tightly contested. The underlying ratings say the teams are not nearly as equal as those records suggest.</p>

<h2 id="power-index" data-source-paragraph="145"><strong>The Dual Eights Power Index: A Different Top 10</strong></h2>

<p data-source-paragraph="146">The Efficiency Index looks backward, measuring actual performance through six weeks.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="147">DEPI tries to estimate future strength.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="148">It incorporates opponent-adjusted performance, historical results, greater weighting of recent games and an adjustment for the expected starting quarterback.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="149">The rating is expressed in points relative to an average NFL team on a neutral field.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="150">A +5 team is approximately five points stronger than average. A -5 team is approximately five points weaker.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="151"><strong>One important methodology update:</strong> DEPI has been refitted for Week 6, with stronger statistical regularization and a more conservative rating scale. Ratings are therefore compressed compared with the original Week 5 model.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="152">San Francisco&#x27;s published rating has moved from +9.86 last week to +8.17, for example, but that is not evidence that the 49ers suddenly deteriorated. Much of the difference comes from the revised formula.</p>

<p data-source-paragraph="153">For meaningful comparisons of weekly performance, the movement discussed here uses the new formula applied retrospectively to Week 5 as well. The live dashboard&#x27;s movement column instead compares against the previously published rankings, so a few positions differ.</p>
